Hamilton rotation gate

Status: ACTIVE (29 Oct 2026; was READY). In the float-trained FBS trunk it is the feed-forward block, trained and served with exact rotations and its DEV export gap inside the bar under lut-stack/5 (claude-182; see the pipeline map). The text below dates from the 256-step micro-rung and is kept as a dated result. The exact forward and backward passes, the GPU kernels, the 120-icosian snap + Q12 i32 export path, and the first 256-step D37 offline-float language-fit and export-gap sheet (#2824) are built and measured on 1 x RTX 5090. Learned quaternion rotation beats the fixed-random-icosian null by +1,010..+1,402 DEV top-1 rows and exports with a 2..23-row (0.014..0.161 pp) gap under hard-forward icosian training (Hamilton_IcosianHard), but trails the un-rotated identity control (IdentityGate_Control) at the 256-step micro-rung budget.

Labels used below: proof means derived from definitions or code, measurement means a recorded run or test, hypothesis means an untested idea. See docs/formal_vocabulary.md.

In plain words

Inside a feed-forward layer, a gate decides how much of a signal passes through. The usual gate squashes a number into the range 0 to 1 with a sigmoid curve, and the project's rules now ban that curve. This gate instead turns a gate signal into a rotation in four dimensions and rotates the matching value by it, four numbers at a time. A rotation only turns a vector. It uses no exponential and no squashing. A consequence worth knowing: a rotation cannot make a vector shorter, so this gate steers the signal rather than switching it off. Whether that is enough is not yet known.

What it replaces in a transformer

The gate in the feed-forward layer (the multilayer perceptron) that follows attention. The common form, SwiGLU, multiplies the value by a sigmoid-shaped gate.

What the swap does and does not do. The rotation keeps the signal's length; the length of the gate block enters only through the backward pass (right, drawn from the formula).

How it works

Work on blocks of four numbers. Let \(g\) be a gate block and \(u\) the matching "up" (value) block of the hidden layer. Compute \[r=\sqrt{|g|^2+10^{-12}},\qquad q=g/r,\qquad \text{out}=q\otimes u,\] where \(\otimes\) is the Hamilton product (the quaternion product) and \(q\otimes u=L(q)\,u\) with the \(4\times4\) matrix \(L\) given on the spin-weights page.

Two facts (proof).

  • Quaternion length is multiplicative, \(|q\otimes u|=|q|\,|u|\), and \(|q|=|g|/\sqrt{|g|^2+10^{-12}}\). So \(|{\rm out}|=|u|\) up to a factor that falls below 1 only when \(|g|\) is near \(10^{-6}\) or smaller (at \(|g|=10^{-6}\) the factor is \(0.71\)). A zero gate block gives \(q=0\) and zero output. The output's length is set by \(u\) alone; the gate sets only its direction.
  • The backward pass is exact, including the normalisation (Definition): \[\partial u=\bar q\otimes d,\qquad \partial q=d\otimes\bar u,\qquad \partial g=\frac{\partial q-q\,(q\cdot\partial q)}{r},\] with \(d\) the incoming adjoint and \(\bar q\) the quaternion conjugate. No exp, sigmoid, tanh or softmax appears in either direction.

The hazard and per-block suppression (Supp_Hamilton, proof). Define intrinsic per-block suppression (Definition, Supp_Hamilton) as achieving amplitude ratio \(\rho(g,u):=\|\text{out}\|_2/\|u\|_2 \le \tau\) (registered at \(\tau=0.5\)) with bounded backward Jacobian norm \(\|\partial q/\partial g\|_2 \le 10\). Because \(\rho(g,u)=\|g\|_2/\sqrt{\|g\|_2^2+10^{-12}}\) and \(\rho^2 + 10^{-12}/r^2 = 1\), we have \(\rho(g,u)\le\tau\) if and only if \(\|g\|_2 \le \frac{\tau}{\sqrt{1-\tau^2}}\times 10^{-6}\) (\(\approx 5.7735\times 10^{-7}\) at \(\tau=0.5\)), where the orthogonal backward scale is \(1/r = 10^6\sqrt{1-\rho^2} \ge \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\times 10^6 \approx 8.6603\times 10^5\) and the radial derivative is \(\frac{d\|q\|_2}{d\|g\|_2} = 10^6(1-\rho^2)^{3/2} \ge 6.4952\times 10^5\) (Guarantee, Status: Structural, geometric_stack_native.rs hamilton_gate_suppression_closed_form_and_backward_hazard_proof). Thus a norm-preserving Hamilton gate cannot suppress per-block amplitude outside the \(10^{-12}\) division-by-zero guard core, and inside that core any amplitude reduction is algebraically locked to a \(\sim 10^6\) backward gradient blow-up. Weight decay can push a block toward zero; the 256-step run checked this and found no guard-core entry. Only full-rung or longer-horizon behavior remains open.

Wiring. The hidden width must be divisible by 4. One GPU thread handles one block. The setting gate=hamilton selects it; the default stays the sigmoid SwiGLU gate so old artifacts load byte for byte. The older exporter refused non-default gates; that is historical, since the FBS trunk now serves its unsnapped exact-unit-quaternion Hamilton form under lut-stack/5 at DEV scope. The same operation is also the output gate of the geometric recurrence.

What the owner's card left open (hypothesis). An icosian snap of \(q\), so the product becomes a read in the 120×120 table; an exact hard forward with a straight-through gradient bridge; and a serving form. The snap and FBS serving form now exist at their declared scopes; fresh transfer and the joined session remain open.

Rule check.

  • D11 (integer serving): the discrete M1 composition includes a discrete Hamilton gate, and the FBS trunk serves its unsnapped exact-unit-quaternion form under lut-stack/5 at DEV scope. The older exporter refusal is historical; the joined FBS session still has open parts.
  • D23 (no softmax or squashing): passes by construction. D25 closed the sigmoid exception that SwiGLU relied on, which this replaces.
  • D25/D30 (no dense matrix multiplication): a per-block Hamilton product is not a dense contraction. The owner permits a geometric feed-forward block of this gate plus Hamilton-block projections, and the projection half has since been replaced by SpiralCore/FBS.
  • D32/D37: a float operation, frozen by D32. D37 lets it return only through a new pre-registered offline sheet (decisions).

Reading these honestly. The 22 October row settles per-block amplitude suppression by proof (Guarantee, Status: Structural), and the 25 October D37 offline-float run (#2824, measurement) settles the empirical training and export-gap questions at the 256-step micro-rung scale: (1) under weight_decay = 0.1, zero of 78,888,960 gate blocks per evaluation ever approach the \(10^{-12}\) guard core (\(\min \|g_b\|_2 = 0.00918\), \(\max 1/r = 108.94\), \(\min \rho = 0.99999971\)), and because QuaternionWeights::SpiralcoreFbs applies an orthogonal Cl(0,6) signed permutation to each routed 8D input atom (two 4-blocks), the downstream projection norm ratio is also identically 1.000000 (0 / 986,112 rows with \(\gamma_{\text{down}} < 0.8\), proof + measurement); (2) hard-forward 120-icosian snapping with a straight-through backward pass (Hamilton_IcosianHard) eliminates the continuous-to-icosian quantization shock (97.59%–97.84% row-wise float-vs-integer top-1 agreement, \(\Delta_{\text{top1}} = 2–23\) rows vs 80–312 rows for post-hoc snapping of Hamilton_Active); and (3) while learned quaternion rotation beats RandomGate_Null by > 1,000 rows and clears TrainUnigram_CountTable (1,226) on both seeds in Build A, it trails the un-rotated IdentityGate_Control (3,041–3,908) by 1,743–2,644 rows at 256 steps because random initial mlp.gate/rec.gate projections scramble the up branch at step 0 (admitted = 5.87%).

What has never been tested

  • Identity-preconditioned or residual-interpolated Hamilton_IcosianHard initialization (starting \(q_b\) at the identity icosian \((1,0,0,0)\) so step 0 matches IdentityGate_Control rather than uniform \(S^3\) scrambling) at longer horizon (>= 1,024 steps).
  • A matched comparison against the frozen pre-ban artifact's stored outputs at full rung scale.
  • Cross-block mixing downstream of Gate::Hamilton (where relative quaternion phases between distinct 4-blocks can cancel in sum, unlike single-atom SpiralcoreFbs routing).
