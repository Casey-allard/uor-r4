UOR-R4

What the project knows

The project's record holds more lessons about what misleads than results about what works. The most useful knowledge is a set of 21 lessons, mostly about how to measure honestly, plus a short list of measured results. The honest summary is this. Several lawful parts exist and have passed their finite tests. No model on the current path has learned language: the best trained discrete model sits below a simple count table. The cause is not yet established.

flowchart TB
  A["Passes with the teacher's text supplied<br/>(teacher-forced)"] --> B["Passes on a panel the authors built"]
  B --> C["Passes on development rows<br/>the team has already seen"]
  C --> D["Passes on fresh held-out rows"]
  D --> E["Writes coherent text on its own"]
  E --> F["Same result from the shipped integer file"]
  F --> G["Faster or cheaper per token on a laptop"]

The ladder a result must climb before it counts as capability. The project has repeatedly stopped at the first three steps (lessons 3 and 4 below).

How to read this page

  • #N is issue or pull request N in UOR-Foundation/uor-r4. Each lesson cites the items it came from.
  • The lessons come from the audit's reading of the GitHub archive, items #1 to #2495. Some items were read in full and some by title only (docs/labs/repo-renormalization-2026-10-19/audit/eras/eras.json, field notes). Check the item before you rely on a number.
  • Each lesson records something that happened at a stated scope, so each is a measurement of that case. Where a lesson generalises, the generalisation is a hypothesis, and it never closes a mechanism (decision D22).
  • Mechanism names use the statuses ACTIVE, READY and PARKED. See the mechanism board.

The 21 lessons, by theme

The numbers are the audit's own, so you can trace a lesson back to its era in the history. Lessons are grouped by theme, so their numbers are not in order; other pages cite them by number.

Check the measurement first

  1. Check the instrument before trusting a result. A metric that read 0.0000 hid a real loss (the #99 metric, removed by the revert #102). An outside check on the same text differed from the pipeline's own score by about a factor of three, 11.17 against 3.84 bits per token (#300). Typed-in numbers were once merged as results (#1459). A lookup-table gain was retracted as a constant shift (#1881). A control that checked zero rows reported a pass (#2112).
  2. Measure the shipped form. The trained model and the shipped artifact differed by 0.57 bits per byte (BPB) (#1284).
  3. A teacher-forced or authored-panel pass is not coherent generation. Loss kept falling while prose stayed at 0 of 5 (#948, #1264, #1398).
  4. Development is not fresh. A frozen-upstream adapter scored 437 of 512 on rows the team had seen, then 0 of 128 on a fresh draw (#2162, #2164).
  5. A gate set before a run does not move after a miss, and a missing input is not a negative. Frozen floors survived every re-entry (#887, #931). An UNAVAILABLE report is not a zero (#661, #986).

What geometry bought and what it did not

  1. Fixed geometry usually tied or lost to a matched ordinary control. This held for W(3,3) (#929), for the project's geometric mechanisms as of September (#1371) and for fixed E8 or H4 codebooks against k-means (#1456). Kept exceptions: quaternion transport (#1460), quaternion and icosian weights (#2193, #2216) and seed reliability (#1798).
  2. Some geometry wins were storage or lexical artifacts. A zeta-grid headline was storage banding (#440); see also #487.
  3. Exact algebra reproduces perfectly but carries no meaning on its own. H4 and the 64-state SpiralCore worked as controls and supplied no semantics (#952, #985).
  4. Shift-and-add stores and E8 codebooks beat sign codes at equal bits (#243, #395).

Data and scale

  1. Data and teacher breadth beat model size and clever stores. On broad text the ceiling was the teacher (#437). Matching the training distribution beat adding parameters (#1913).

Memory and retrieval

  1. An exact log plus a prime sieve retrieves; the learned pointer copied recency. The pointer returned the most recent value, not the queried one (#1558). The sieve did the retrieval (#1559, #1607).
  2. Chat failures came from missing knowledge and fluency, not retrieval. Retrieval accounted for 2.2% of failing rows (#1708).
  3. Compliance needs a demand paired with its exact short answer. Instruction following was 0 of 6 until each demand was paired (#1918, #1958).
  4. Exact integer export and bit-exact serving work (#1254, #1893).

Learning

  1. Hard geometric selection gives unselected keys no gradient. It needs a training bridge (#2195, #2249).
  2. State and horizon were the levers, not training length (#1366, #1379).
  3. Binding failures came from the training recipe, not the architecture. A copied reference cell scored 99.17% where the bundled recipe failed (#1045, #1050).

Speed and cost

  1. Savings counted on paper are not tokens per second. Analytic multiply savings did not become speed (#2200, #2211). Integer serving first cost 4.3 times more energy than its float parent (on a dense model with every multiply replaced by a table read) (#1449).
  2. Check where compute goes. The output readout took 99.97% of per-token compute (#1287). Geometric operations were 0.05% of the stack's arithmetic (#2186).

How to work

  1. Keep dormant mechanisms behind gates; never delete them (#515, #1747).
  2. A process that forbids running code produces nothing (#1111). A negative closes a configuration, never a mechanism (#2165).
