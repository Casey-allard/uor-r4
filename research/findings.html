UOR-R4

What the project knows

The project now has one measured milestone result beside its longer record of measurement lessons: M1 is complete. A saved discrete model beats both count tables on two seeds through its integer export, on DEV, Fresh V2 and held-out phrasing. It is not useful chat. The broader lesson remains: the difference between a construction check, a panel result and a working product is where most mistakes happen.

flowchart TB
  A["Passes with the teacher's text supplied<br/>(teacher-forced)"] --> B["Passes on a panel the authors built"]
  B --> C["Passes on development rows<br/>the team has already seen"]
  C --> D["Passes on fresh held-out rows"]
  D --> E["Writes coherent text on its own"]
  E --> F["Same result from the shipped integer file"]
  F --> G["Faster or cheaper per token on a laptop"]

M1 reaches the saved integer-file panel result. It does not by itself establish coherent generation, full product parity or cost.

How to read this page

  • #N is issue or pull request N in UOR-Foundation/uor-r4. Each lesson cites its source.
  • The 21 lessons came from the audit's reading of items up to #2495. Newer M1 and generation evidence is listed separately below.
  • Each lesson is a measurement at a stated scope; a generalisation is a hypothesis. A negative closes a configuration, never a mechanism.
  • Mechanism names use ACTIVE, READY and PARKED. See the mechanism board.

The 21 lessons, by theme

Check the measurement first

  1. Check the instrument before trusting a result. A metric that read 0.0000 hid a real loss (#99/#102), an outside check differed by about three times (#300), typed-in numbers were once merged as results (#1459), and a zero-row control once reported a pass (#2112).
  2. Measure the shipped form. The trained model and shipped artifact differed by 0.57 BPB (#1284). The M1 close therefore uses the integer UORLUT01 artifact, not only a training score.
  3. Teacher-forced or authored-panel success is not coherent generation. Loss fell while prose stayed at 0 of 5 (#948, #1264, #1398).
  4. Development is not fresh. A frozen-upstream adapter scored 437/512 on seen rows, then 0/128 on a fresh draw (#2162, #2164).
  5. A gate set before a run does not move after a miss, and a missing input is not a negative. Frozen floors survived re-entry (#887, #931), and UNAVAILABLE is not zero (#661, #986).

What geometry bought and what it did not

  1. Fixed geometry usually tied or lost to a matched ordinary control. This held for W(3,3) (#929), September's mechanisms (#1371), and fixed E8/H4 codebooks against k-means (#1456). Kept exceptions were quaternion transport (#1460), quaternion/icosian weights (#2193, #2216) and seed reliability (#1798). M1 is a whole-model result, not proof of each mechanism independently.
  2. Some geometry wins were storage or lexical artifacts. A zeta-grid headline was storage banding (#440; also #487).
  3. Exact algebra reproduces perfectly but carries no meaning on its own. H4 and the 64-state SpiralCore supplied controls, not semantics (#952, #985).
  4. Shift-and-add stores and E8 codebooks beat sign codes at equal bits (#243, #395).

Data and scale

  1. Data and teacher breadth beat model size and clever stores. On broad text the ceiling was the teacher (#437), and matching the training distribution beat adding parameters (#1913).

Memory and retrieval

  1. An exact log plus a prime sieve retrieves; the learned pointer copied recency. The pointer returned the most recent value (#1558); the sieve did the retrieval (#1559, #1607). The newer true-address oracle is 80/80 against 0/80 no-memory and 20/80 sieve, but that is still oracle scope.
  2. Chat failures came from missing knowledge and fluency, not only retrieval. Retrieval accounted for 2.2% of failing rows in the older analysis (#1708).
  3. Compliance needs a demand paired with its exact short answer. Instruction following was 0 of 6 until each demand was paired (#1918, #1958).
  4. Exact integer export and bit-exact serving work (#1254, #1893), and the M1 pair has exact 14,287-row DEV export agreement with B4 pass at both seeds.

Learning

  1. Hard geometric selection gives unselected keys no gradient. It needs a training bridge (#2195, #2249).
  2. State and horizon were the levers, not training length (#1366, #1379).
  3. Binding failures came from the training recipe, not the architecture. A copied reference cell scored 99.17% where the bundled recipe failed (#1045, #1050).

Speed and cost

  1. Savings counted on paper are not tokens per second. Analytic multiply savings did not become speed (#2200, #2211). Integer serving first cost 4.3 times more energy than float on a dense model (#1449).
  2. Check where compute goes. The output readout took 99.97% of per-token compute (#1287), and geometric operations were 0.05% of an older stack's arithmetic (#2186).

How to work

  1. Keep dormant mechanisms behind gates; never delete them (#515, #1747).
  2. A process that forbids running code produces nothing (#1111). A negative closes a configuration, never a mechanism (#2165).
