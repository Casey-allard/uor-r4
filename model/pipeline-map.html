UOR-R4

The pipeline map

This page follows one token from the training data to the last word of a reply served on an Apple M1 laptop. For each stage it gives seven things, cost first:

  • its cost per token, measured where a profile exists;
  • the mechanism;
  • the exact domain it reads and the domain it writes, and whether the next stage reads that domain;
  • its size now and the size the goal needs;
  • its hardware;
  • its status;
  • what it depends on.

It answers the owner's direction of 27 October 2026 (recorded verbatim in the item pipeline-map). The goal is a local model of about GPT-4's ability, trained on less than a datacenter and served on a laptop like this one.

This revision (31 Oct) retains claude-159's cost-per-token map and adds:

  • claude-149 to claude-158, as listed under what changed since the first map;
  • the owner's 28 Oct criterion: "I don't care what we serve at long as it is cheap";
  • the saved discrete M1 close and claude-211's bar-f result, and claude-217's current new-phrasing opening failure;
  • a section on the archived prime and angular router.

The page ends with:

  • the scale arithmetic for the goal;
  • the gaps, ranked;
  • an order of work.

Labels.

  • Measurement: a recorded run; its source is named.
  • Proof: follows from the code or from arithmetic on stated numbers.
  • Hypothesis: untested.
  • External: a published figure from outside the project, with the publication named under Sources outside the project.

Code line numbers are at 5bc6f6522. Float-training notebook rows are named by unit and date, because the notebook adds new rows at the top.

The hardware this page targets (measurement, sysctl on this laptop, 27 Oct; the same machine as claude-126's timing, line 73, and claude-158's profile):

  • Apple M1, with 4 performance and 4 efficiency cores;
  • 16 GiB of memory (17,179,869,184 bytes);
  • macOS 27.

The M1's other units are an 8-core GPU and a 16-core Neural Engine (external, Apple). Training runs on one RTX 5090 per pod.

  • the tokenizer and the data;

4. Trunk

FieldNow
Cost per tokenServing, measured (claude-158): 3.68 ms of a 3.73 ms step on the M1, 98.6%. That is 613 µs a layer: the recurrence block (three route gathers around the recurrence core) 352–364 µs, and the feed-forward block (three gathers, the gated product and the requantization) 253–259 µs. That is about 57 ns per learnable value, scalar integer code with no NEON. Which part of each block dominates is not split. Training, measured (claude-157): the forward is 4.8 ms of a 96.5 ms step at width 256 (5%), and 6.9 of 79.0 ms at width 512 (9%). Each recurrence is 0.55–0.84 ms of GPU time, and its host time is 0.99 of that: host-bound by the sheet's rule. The layer right after the stream's read is the exception, at 1.69 ms of host time. The trunk's backward is inside the backward (62% / 54%), not split.
MechanismThe FBS sheet, all merged. hamilton_sheet.rs lines 61–72 and 1935–1953, geometric_stack_native.rs lines 101, 344, 479 and 687, spiralcore_fbs.rs lines 26–28 and 257:
  • six recurrence layers (pattern rrrrrr);
  • width 256, as 64 quaternion lanes;
  • 4 heads;
  • a 384-wide inner block;
  • a context of 192;
  • the Hamilton gate, with geometric recurrence gates and quaternion rotation;
  • a convolution of width 4;
  • six maps per layer, rec.in, rec.gate, rec.out and the three mlp maps (shapes(), line 687), each a SpiralCore/FBS projection of shape [out/8, stages, 8] in place of a dense matrix (QUATERNION_MAPS, line 479; line 344).
The saved discrete M1 model combines Flock FrozenZeroV1, DiscreteHamiltonGateV1, DiscreteQuaternionRecurrenceV1, PositionHolonomyV1, OrbitRootSnapNormV1, an orbit-root causal pointer and post-rotation OuterL1V1; the earlier Route/Relation reads are Stage T history, not the closing composition (M1 close).
DomainOne table point per position in; a state in ℝ²⁵⁶ after the final norm out. The readout reads it as a query point in the table's space: yes. What the state carries: replacing the token k places back with BOS moves the opening point by 5.97 at k = 0, 0.37 at k = 6 and 0.04 at k = 12. 57–66% of lane positions in layers 1–5 sit at the shortest memory rate after training, against 33% at step 0. Both measurements: float-training, row of 27 Oct "Exploration".
SizeNow: 64,000 learnable values in the six layers and the final norm (claude-155, "The pivot"). Each layer has six FBS maps holding 8,448 values, with 4 stages for inputs of 256 and 384, plus 2,176 norm, convolution, gate and decay values (proof by arithmetic on shapes(), line 687). With the table, the model holds 1,112,576 values, 94% of them the table. The state does not grow with context (proof: a recurrence). Width 512 is the best fit measured (Status, below). Goal: Phi-3-mini has 3.8B parameters (external), about 3,400 times this whole model.
HardwareTraining: the current FBS implementation and every qualification run remain GPU-only; the model refuses CUDA-free execution in F32 with SpiralCore/FBS, with "no host or dense fallback" (geometric_stack_native.rs line 938). D84's separate CPU research module is development-only and does not change this current model path. Serving: the integer runtime on the M1 CPU, scalar code. Native NEON table kernels exist only for an older frozen stack (desktop client line 49). The M1 GPU and Neural Engine are unused.
StatusFit is measured: whole-table nearest top-1 is 9,328 / 10,573 at width 256 with the cell objective (claude-152), and 10,152 / 11,140 at width 512 (claude-153). Storage is measured short: the trunk holds 64,000 values beside a 1,048,576-value table. Openings never beat claude-152's 302.0 / 228.0 by the sheet's margin (bar 347.3), where a lookup of the prompt's first 8 tokens names 607 of 1,226 (claude-155, "The pivot"). 5. Look-back

FieldNow
Cost per tokenTraining, measured (claude-157): the two Hamming reads, in the stream after layer 2 and on the readout, cost 7.9 ms each in the forward of a 96.5 ms step (16% together). They are GPU-bound (host time 0.86 of GPU time), and the same cost at both places. Their backward is inside the backward. Rates with the bag 28,560 / 21,430 and with the learned register 12,573 / 12,641 context tok/s, against (a)'s 32,049 with the Hamming read alone; these ran on different hosts, so the gap is a bound (claude-155). Serving: none, because the integer runtime has no read. By arithmetic (from the read's shapes): 4 heads × 64 bits is 32 bytes of code per position per read. With reads at two points, a token costs 8N popcounts and reads 64N bytes at context N. At N = 32,768 that is 2 MiB per token. A dense model's cache, at Llama-2-7B's shape (32 layers, width 4,096, 16-bit keys and values; external), is 512 KiB per position, or 16 GiB per token at the same N: 8,192 times as many bytes (proof).
MechanismMerged (claude-146, #3332): the Hamming read.
  • each earlier position's key is the sign code of its token's table point after a learned per-lane rotation, 64 bits per head, with 4 heads (8 at width 512);
  • the match is popcount of XOR, a hard top 16, weighted by max(0, m − d)/m;
  • copy heads move the readout point n toward the copied token's point c: n + w(c − n).
Variants since:
  • type spin, through SpiralCore's quaternion group: negative, with seed 2 passing the openings bar only (claude-151);
  • the opening read, the mean of the turn's first 8 points after layer 2: negative, and ignored, since its removal changes 25 / 19 tokens (claude-154);
  • the plain bag of the user's turn (one slot, uniform over the span, by a doubling scan): it meets in-space-trunk-to-text's done-when. Dropping it costs 1,514 / 1,648 tokens and 189 / 236 openings (claude-155);
  • the learned prompt register: collapses training (T 684 / 1,246), with untested causes: keys bent through the live stream, and slot starts that coincide in groups (claude-155).
Earlier, negative against their bars: the state-keyed Flock read (claude-144) and the content read (claude-145).
DomainSign codes in {0,1}⁶⁴ per head; out, a moved readout point in ℝ²⁵⁶. The readout reads it: yes. With the cell objective, the read names 1,517 / 2,933 of 4,130 copied rows (claude-152). At width 512 the same trunk without the read copies about a quarter of them (claude-153). Points added inside the stream are ignored: the opening read (claude-154), and the two earlier copy forms, whose payload "reads to the trunk as its own input" and "barely moves where the prediction lands" (claude-146 lines 29–30).
SizeNow: a context of 192 (SHEET_CONTEXT, hamilton_sheet.rs line 66). The serving session cannot slide its window (fbs-toks.rs line 9). The discrete path's window holds at most 243 = 3⁵ positions (architecture, stage 3). Goal: GPT-4 shipped with 8,192 and 32,768 tokens of context (external), 43 to 171 times this.
HardwareTraining: GPU. Serving: none. The integer runtime has no Hamming read and no bag.
StatusCopies measured positive; openings measured flat. The bag carries the turn into the trunk, and the trunk cannot store what to do with it (stage 4). Since 27 Oct: claude-189 built the copy heads' integer kernel, which matches an f64 reference (claude-189); the served session does not use it yet.

6. Long memory

FieldNow
Cost per tokenPer read: 384 bytes and 8 fingerprint checks for a small range (measurement, object store line 46). How many reads a token needs is undefined while no stage issues them. By arithmetic: a lookup keyed by the turn's first k ids is a hash and a load, far below the trunk's 3.68 ms. This is the form route-plus-memory ran (claude-161), with no measured training cost.
MechanismThe object store, merged (object store). A record is a scale byte, a 12-byte fingerprint and a 32-byte leaf (line 23). A range read authenticates only the branches it touches (line 31). The E8 orbit memory is PARKED, at about 59 MB per layer (e8-orbit-memory line 34). The archived prime routes (gcd admission over n-let keys) are in the archived router.
DomainAn exact address in; stored bytes out. For the object store, no stage reads its output or forms its input.
  • With the true address supplied by the oracle, the answer was right 80 of 80 times, against 20 of 80 for the deterministic sieve and 0 of 80 with no memory (PLAN, the exact-memory premise).
  • Free-running with an exact hint recovered 0 of 40 (exact memory line 11).
  • The evidence for keying it by the turn: TRAIN's commonest opening per first-8-token key names 607 of DEV's 1,226 openings. TRAIN has 212,968 openings over 26,450 distinct prompt starts (claude-154, claude-155). claude-154's turn anchor already forms that key exactly at every opening.
SizeNow: a 23-byte range of a 32,768-byte object reads 8 nodes, 384 bytes (measurement, object store line 46). A record stores 32 bytes in 45, about 1.4 times the payload (proof). Goal: hold facts in place of parameters, at whatever size a laptop disk allows (hypothesis).
HardwareCPU, memory and disk, on both the GPU host and the M1. No GPU work.
StatusThe store is measured, and for the opening it is now connected (claude-161). Exact turn memory keyed by the user's first 8 tokens names 598.7 / 598.3 of DEV's 1,226 reply openings at two seeds, against 266.3 / 281.0 for an empty-table control and 302.0 / 228.0 for the prior model (ceiling 607). DEV top-1 is 9,490 / 10,368. The opening table moves the first word, and the completed reply table extends later rows. The /7 engine serves both tables on synthetic fixtures; trained-artifact parity and memory-session restore remain open.
Depends onUpstream: an address, which the turn anchor can form (claude-154). Downstream: the readout. The look-back's lesson applies: a stored token must land as its table point where the readout reads.

7. Readout

FieldNow

The archived prime and angular router

The owner, 28 Oct: "it is worth looking into the archived hopf golden ratio complex - riemannian bridge we used for the original prime router or angular router". This section reads it from the files the archive holds.

What it was, in three generations:

  1. The ancestor, a Python "R4 Prime Router" (server.py, README; evidence note).
    • Word vectors (GloVe blended with geometric coordinates) lie on a hypersphere, and a query is the sum of its words.
    • A brain state evolves as H ← normalize(γ·H + (1−γ)·S_query) (README).
    • Retrieval scores shared prime factors ×100, then cosine, then a window slice norm.
    • The Hopf coordinates (χ, δ, α) and the top sentence went into the system prompt of a local Ollama model (gemma4:e2b).
    • The "Riemannian Hopf canvas" is its 3-D stereographic view of the zeta rings.
    • Its next word is a greedy argmax over VOCAB_MATRIX @ S, which is a dense product. Above temperature 0, one path samples from a normalized exponential, which is banned.
  2. ADR-0003, fixed-zeta prime routes (ADR; the pointer at archive/adr/0003-… names a moved path).
    • Zeta phases: θ_j(p) = wrap(γ_j·log p) per prime atom, with a compiled phase-delta table at runtime (§1).
    • Semiprime transition experts: e_t = p_(t−1)·p_t, with gcd(e_t, e_(t+1)) = p_t (§2).
    • An n-let context key: the product of the last k primes.
    • An S³ compute state with its Hopf observation h ∈ S², the fibre phase kept as torsion (§3).
    • The choice rule: least integer energy over a few index rows.
    • §5, the direction-preserving golden radial bridge. This is the "golden ratio complex–Riemannian bridge" the owner remembers. With E : ℂ² → ℝ⁴ the ordinary identification, R_φ(z) = φ·E(z), and φ = (1 + √5)/2. It preserves direction exactly: angle, prime direction and Hopf sector are unchanged, and only the radial shell moves. In ℤ[φ], a shell step is the integer Fibonacci step (a, b)·φ → (b, a + b): one add, or one subtraction for φ⁻¹. The map is from ℂ², not ℂ⁴.
  3. The prime-route pointer (1–2 Oct, 70d8a223c #1587, f9c926925 #1589).
    • Each token has a registered prime, and a position's key is the product of its last W tokens' distinct primes.
    • A source is admitted when it shares a factor with the query (gcd > 1).
    • Admitted sources are scored by ln gcd plus recency, then by a normalized exponential, which is now banned. The ranked variant adds a learned score.

What was measured (each a measurement, source named):

  • The ancestor: angular routing and decodability only, never generation, and not reproduced under today's gates. Verdict: inconclusive (hopf-observation, "What has been tried").

  • Hopf sector routing:

    • #303: 7 of 512 sectors occupied;
    • #306, after a fixed signed projection: 43 of 512;
    • #430: as a retrieval filter, MRR 0.0045 against 0.0743, no retrieval value.

    Sector routing is PARKED; the kept-fibre map is READY (D72, hopf-observation).

  • ADR-0003 as a whole: RETAIN_STORAGE_RECALL_ONLY (qualification). The product probe never ran: there was no prompt-to-address compiler, no address-to-token decoder and no generation loop. The learned four-coordinate mixer before it "selected support but did not transport adequate semantic value": a 4.44% real-against-permuted held-out advantage against a 5% gate (#951, recorded on #958).

  • The prime-ranked pointer (10 Oct, pointer-key): the hit rate rose from 0.377 to 0.545. Memory answers stayed at 19, 21 and 23 of 40, against a bar of 25. The intended key, entity prime × attribute prime, was never built.

  • The choice rule: on PR #455's context-class test (not the router), among the latent arms hard selection got 22.81% top-1, top-3 24.54% and averaging over all 25.91%. Hard selection did not win. The test's own fixed baseline, at 26.42%, was above all three.

  • The golden bridge (§5): built and tested as algebra (the ℤ[φ] steps are IMPLEMENTED_SUBSTRATE and pass their source-free fixtures, PASS_SOURCE_FREE, ADR-0003 and the qualification record). It has no language result, and no rule ties a zeta phase or n-let to a shell ("bound only as structural transport", ADR-0003).

  • The exact-memory sieve: 20 of 80 against 80 of 80 with the true address, and 0 of 80 with no memory (stage 6).

Proposal for the owner's queue

This is only a proposal. No §3 constraint is relaxed here, and this page waits on no answer.

The multiplier-free rule (D11, until D80 and D81 amended CHARTER §3.6 on 28 Oct 2026) had one measured cost: 4.3 times the energy, on an older dense model (stage 11). claude-158's profile puts 98.6% of a served FBS token in the trunk's scalar kernels. It does not split the share spent in table-emulated products inside them. If the owner wants that share as a number, R3's per-layer split can report it.

Sources outside the project

  • Apple, M1 announcement (November 2020): 8-core GPU at 2.6 TFLOPS; 16-core Neural Engine at 11 trillion operations per second. LPDDR4X-4266 on a 128-bit bus gives 4,266 MT/s × 16 bytes ≈ 68.3 GB/s.
  • OpenAI, GPT-4 Technical Report (2023): no parameter count or training data size is disclosed. The March 2023 announcement offered 8,192 and 32,768 tokens of context.
  • OpenAI, tiktoken: the cl100k_base encoding used by GPT-4 has 100,256 tokens.
  • Touvron et al., Llama 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models (2023): the 7B model has 32 layers and width 4,096, with one key and one value per head.
  • Llama Team, The Llama 3 Herd of Models (2024): the 405B model trained on 15.6T tokens with 3.8 × 10²⁵ FLOPs.
  • Abdin et al., Phi-3 Technical Report (2024): Phi-3-mini has 3.8B parameters, trained on 3.3T tokens, and scores 69% on MMLU.
  • Hoffmann et al., Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (2022): about 20 training tokens per parameter.
  • Allen-Zhu and Li, Physics of Language Models: Part 3.3, Knowledge Capacity Scaling Laws (2024): about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter.
