UOR-R4

The model, end to end

UOR-R4 turns text into whole-number token ids, carries those ids through geometric state and exact memory, and chooses the next token without softmax or dense matrix multiplication. M1 is complete: a saved discrete model passed all six bars on two seeds. No model holds a useful conversation. Two model paths exist and have not joined: the discrete M1 path and the float-trained FBS trunk.

The upper discrete M1 lane and lower FBS research lane are different models, not one joined pipeline; only the D11 integer serving boundary is shared.

Labels follow docs/formal_vocabulary.md: proof, measurement, hypothesis. A construction control is not a language result, and NOT_RUN is never a pass.

The discrete path that closed M1

The saved artifact is trunk-joint-pointer-v1-d11, a 19-table uor-r4.lut-discrete-trunk/1 UORLUT01 model. Its composition is Flock FrozenZeroV1, a discrete Hamilton gate, discrete quaternion recurrence, PositionHolonomyV1, OrbitRootSnapNormV1, a four-block orbit-root causal pointer, and post-rotation outer-L1 readout.

Current failure and latest probe. Claude-217's cycle and echo steps end every registered loop, but content is underpowered and greedy remains the decode. On p-s, teacher-forced reply openings are 17.4%, 13.7% and 18.6% at three seeds, against 80.8%, 80.2% and 79.9% on later tokens. Exact-reference replies are only 3, 1 and 4 of 256, and exact-key memory finds little on new phrasing (claude-217). Claude-218 found trunk-state keys offline; claude-219 then tested them in free generation on fresh p-v. The point-key opening improves templates by +13/+8/+12 and exact replies by +9/+8/+12 at three seeds, but only partially. The sign key is inconclusive at this panel size. Claude-220 then served the point key inside one float model on fresh p-w: templates improve by +41/+40/+42 at three seeds with exact claude-219 parity, while recalling TRAIN replies rather than generalizing. Claude-222 has built the integer point table, search and /8 record, with 380 library tests and a full serving audit. Claude-224 has built the panel decode and grade. Claude-227 passed retention GPU parity within the 3% bar; Claude-228 built the run's reference/read-back/BOS checks; Claude-229 found an instrument fault: the integer session uses the native writer instead of the trained whole-table nearest readout, so no bar result or throughput is reported. Claude-231 built the nearest readout and export on native and browser paths; claude-232 then missed greedy parity in every cell, so the mismatch must be localized before the p-x rerun; useful chat remains unproven.

What remains unbuilt or unqualified

  • M2 and M3: complete correct replies and durable memory, not just next-token scores.
  • M4: the accepted model's full UORLUT01 export, quality, session and audit path. A lawful likelihood (PRLL) exists, but GPU held-out scoring remains open.
  • A joined model: the discrete M1 path and FBS trunk do not share a table, trunk or served session.
  • M5: whole-path latency, memory traffic and energy; arithmetic counts alone do not establish them.
  • M7: library/CLI/browser parity on the accepted model, not only a synthetic stand-in.
  • Learned address formation: the true-address 80/80 oracle is exact-read evidence, not proof that the model has learned to form addresses or earned M3.

Historical K16, kept in scope

K16-A scored 65 and 463 of 14,287, below unigram 1,226 and T2 6,300. K16-R collapsed to 19 or 20 occupied cells. Those negative configurations remain important evidence, but they predate the M1 close. The older address-key, read, ranking and schedule hypotheses belong to that history; they do not erase the later saved model's result (K16 outcome).
