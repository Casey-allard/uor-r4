UOR-R4

How the model learns

The discrete model learns by trying every legal geometric move of a table entry and keeping a move only when the integer loss strictly improves. The method is exact and runs on the GPU. It has now produced a saved model that passes M1 on two seeds. Earlier failures remain useful negative evidence, not the current best. A separate FBS path trains offline in float under D37.

Discrete learning: exact counterfactual moves, strict acceptance and verification on the GPU.

Labels follow docs/formal_vocabulary.md: proof, measurement, hypothesis. The discrete-learning mechanism page carries the longer history.

The discrete learning step

A vector block is one of four 8-number blocks in a token's vector. A route frame is one of 64 signed-axis rotations. A gate takes values −1, 0 or +1. A vector block has 16 legal states, including "stay as it is" (architecture).

For each candidate move \(m\), the machine replays the read and writer and computes the integer rank-margin loss:

\[L(m)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal R}\ell\big(x;\ \theta\leftarrow m\big),\qquad m\in\{\mathrm{id},m_1,\dots,m_{15}\}.\]

It chooses the lowest loss, with ties going to identity and then deterministic move order. It applies the winner only if \(L(m^*)<L(\mathrm{id})\), and re-checks the stored table. Every accepted move is therefore legal and strictly improves its scored set; that alone says nothing about other rows or fresh transfer.

One 16,404-batch pass scores 1,049,856 targets out of 2,425,925, or 43.3% of the training split (proof by arithmetic). This is why a batch improvement is not by itself a language result.

Row-parallel and BVG-K execution

The ordinary counterfactual evaluates 16 lanes in one block. The row-parallel form evaluates 64 rows × 16 lanes as 1,024 threads and reproduces the ordinary loss on 1,085 finite cases (rows parity). On one RTX 5090, its non-gating diagnostic was about 56 times faster.

BVG-K (batched verified coordinates) scores several coordinates per launch and commits the block only if its joint loss beats the best single move. BVG-K is not merely a construction test: Stage T training (#2836) and the later 19-table joint-pointer Hamilton trunk are part of the M1 lineage. The exact export then reproduces the saved discrete model on all 14,287 DEV rows.

The result that closed M1

The saved 19-table uor-r4.lut-discrete-trunk/1 model scores:

  • DEV 7,002 / 7,001 against unigram 1,226 and T2 6,300;
  • Fresh V2 7,215 / 7,228 against unigram 1,279 and T2 6,582;
  • p-f 7,850 / 7,872 against unigram 1,409 and T2 7,375, beyond paired band 81;
  • Apple M1 decode 10,876 / 10,566 tok/s under the bar-f protocol.

The saved model reads new prompt phrasings into known reply kinds. It does not show new reply kinds or useful conversation; 62.5% of p-f's reference replies are verbatim TRAIN replies (M1 close, Fresh V2, bar-f report).

Offline float training under D37

The separate FBS trunk uses float forward passes, gradients and an optimizer offline, in its own scanned module. Softmax, squashing and dense matmul remain banned. Every float candidate must report its export gap on the same evaluator, and the shipped artifact must be integer.

Since claude-179, the FBS backward uses a fixed-order reduction, so two runs give the same model byte for byte. The lut-stack/5 export is inside the gap bar at DEV scope. Determinism is not a quality claim: on new phrasing, the model still starts replies poorly (claude-217).

Pre-registration and runs

Before GPU training or evaluation, and before any run a milestone claim rests on, a one-page sheet states the bar, noise, controls, seeds, cost and what each outcome decides. A non-author reviews it for TEST FITNESS: FIT; a fresh-context subagent counts. Builds, finite kernel tests and host checks need no sheet.

Since D67, a GPU or training run goes ahead on the lab's own whole-project pre-run review inside the caps. There is no blanket owner release for every run; D41's donor pass is the named exception that still needs the owner at dispatch. Pods go through scripts/pod/uor-pod, with at most 4 pods and $8/h across all labs, and a healthy run is not cancelled for a self-set budget.

What is still open

  • Reply opening on new phrasing. Claude-217 measures teacher-forced reply openings of 17.4%, 13.7% and 18.6% on p-s, against about 80% on later tokens. Exact-key memory misses reworded prompts.
  • Independent mechanism effects. M1 is a whole-model result, not an ablation proving each read, gate, route or memory mechanism independently.
  • Complete generation and M2/M3. Next-token ranking is not complete correct replies or durable memory.
  • Cost and scale. Whole-path training and serving costs, model scaling and M5 energy are not established by one passing artifact or by arithmetic projections.
