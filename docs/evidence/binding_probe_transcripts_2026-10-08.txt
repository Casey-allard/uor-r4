UOR-R4 — Binding probe, natural condition: the acknowledgement loop is self-conditioned and retrieval is partly intact
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 149-153). DeepSeek session. References #820.

WHAT RAN
  The expert review's P1: a binding probe over the four rows that differ ONLY in the value inside turn 1
  (I have two cats / I have five cats, both asked "How many cats do I have?"; My name is Ana / My name
  is Ben, both asked "What is my name?"), scoring existing checkpoints with no training. Checkpoints:
  arm-t2 (19.9 M, two-turn demand store), arm-as (19.9 M, attrshort store), arm-bind (33.8 M), and
  arm-w60 (7.16 M, NO demand store - the control in which the record shows binding previously worked).
  The probe records the model's own turn-1 output, its turn-2 reply, and CLASSIFIES each failure into
  the pre-registered categories (binding swap / unreached / readout). Artifacts on the pod:
  bindprobe/{transcripts.md, rows_selftext_eos_arm-*.json, checks.tsv}.

WHAT THE NATURAL-CONDITION TRANSCRIPTS SHOW

  arm-as (attrshort store) - THE ACKNOWLEDGEMENT LOOP IS SELF-CONDITIONED
    cats2   the model's OWN turn 1: " Noted."    -> turn-2 reply: " Noted."    class c_readout
    cats5   the model's OWN turn 1: " Noted."    -> turn-2 reply: " I see."    class c_readout
    The model generates the store's acknowledgement as turn 1 and then answers the follow-up with
    ANOTHER acknowledgement. The failure is a register loop that the mixture itself created: "Noted."
    and "I see." are the hard-coded turn-1 answers of every count/attribute family in the attrshort
    generator, and the model reproduces them in both slots.

  arm-t2 (two-turn store, no acknowledgement family) - RETRIEVAL IS PARTLY INTACT, READOUT IS NOT
    ana     reply: " My name is Ana brain."                      class c_readout
    ben     reply: " My name is Reflection, and I'm..."          class c_readout
    cats5   reply: " My favorite pet is the most popular pet..." class b_unreached
    The ana row RETRIEVES THE VALUE - "Ana" is in the reply - and still fails to answer the question,
    which is the readout failure downstream of retrieval that the review predicted from the earlier
    "My cat," observation. With a store whose turn-1 answers are prose rather than acknowledgements,
    the model's own turn-1 outputs are prose too (" Hey 1. Balance is a beautiful day..."), so the
    loop and the retrieval failure are separable by the mixture's register.

WHY THIS MATTERS BEFORE THE PINNED MARGINS ARRIVE
  1. THE LEADING MECHANISM HAS DIRECT OBSERVATIONAL SUPPORT. The review's proposal P1 was designed to
     discriminate self-conditioning from a missing span mechanism, and the natural condition already
     shows the self-conditioning signature in the arm whose store supplies acknowledgements, while the
     arm whose store does not shows partial retrieval with a readout failure instead.
  2. IT EXPLAINS WHY EVERY STORE IMPROVEMENT FAILED. Rounds 134, 136 and 143 each made the store
     "better" - longer premises, dilution, short premises at 70 % less mass - and each kept or
     reintroduced the acknowledgement register in the turn-1 slot, so each taught the model the same
     self-conditioned loop. The stores were never the variable; the REGISTER of their turn-1 answers
     was.
  3. IT NAMES THE NEXT DATA CHANGE PRECISELY: turn-1 answers must be CONTENT-BEARING short copies of
     the premise ("Metals: iron, copper, zinc." -> "Iron, copper, zinc.") rather than content-free
     acknowledgements, so the turn-1 slot teaches span selection instead of a register. That is the
     review's P4, and it is cheap: one generator change and one arm.
  4. IT ALSO CORRECTS THE STORE'S OWN VALIDATION, as the review found: the "transformation fraction
     1.000000" checks the answer against turn 1's ANSWER but never against turn 1's PREMISE, where the
     value usually sits, so no store built tonight ever forced the model to ignore the premise.
