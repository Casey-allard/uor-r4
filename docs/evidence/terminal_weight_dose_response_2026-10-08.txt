UOR-R4 — Terminal weighting is non-monotone: 20x moves yes/no compliance, 200x destroys it
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 99-101). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE DOSES
  Same shape (7.16 M, rrarra), same corpus, mask, policy=full_prefix, steps and seed. The only
  difference is UOR_TERMINAL_WEIGHT, which weights the response's terminating <|eos|> - the position
  the verified mask identifies - against ordinary response tokens.

    weight   dev response nll (step 2,000)   first-two-content-token observations
    20       2.1969                          yes/no compiles ("Yes, the"), retention responds
                                             ("My name"), one-word/number do not comply
    200      3.0944                          yes/no REVERTS ("The sky"), retention BREAKS
                                             ("The main"), list artifacts return
    60       2.3508                          running

WHAT THE REVERSAL SHOWS
  1. THE INTERVENTION IS REAL AND TUNABLE. At 20x, two of three yes/no turns open with "Yes" - the
     first compliance movement any arm in this session produced - and at 200x the same class reverts
     to its pre-intervention behaviour. A quantity that can be moved up and then back down by one
     parameter is a working knob, not noise.
  2. IT IS NON-MONOTONE. More terminal weight is not better: at 200x the loss is dominated by stop
     tokens, yes/no compliance disappears, retention conditioning disappears ("The main" where the
     20x arm answered "My name"), and list artifacts reappear in the text ("1. Apple Music Chef's a
     saucepan..."). The optimum is interior, and the useful range lies between these two doses.
  3. THE GUARDS DID THEIR JOB. Fluency and the retention pairs were named in advance as the guard for
     exactly this failure mode - a weight high enough to make the model stop everywhere - and both
     fired at 200x. That is a pre-registered guard working rather than a post-hoc rationalisation.
  4. DEVELOPMENT NLL TRACKED THE DOSE here (2.20, 2.35, 3.09 rising with the weight) without tracking
     quality: the arm with the BEST compliance (20x) does not have the lowest loss. It remains a
     dose indicator and not a metric, consistent with the four earlier arms where it failed to track
     behaviour.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE SEARCH
  The question has narrowed from "can anything move instruction compliance" - answered yes, by this
  change - to "which terminal weight maximises compliance without losing what the model already
  does". That is a one-parameter search with a fixed instrument (panel v2, first two content tokens,
  echo item removed), a stated guard (fluency and the retention pairs) and a known bad region
  (200x). It is cheap: each dose is one arm of the same recipe.

SCOPE
  Three doses, one shape, one corpus, one mask, single seed each, greedy decoding, one panel. Doses
  are single measurements rather than a fitted curve, and the 60x arm is still training; no claim is
  made about the optimum beyond its being interior to [20, 200].
