UOR-R4 — The served gap, settled on identical positions: 0.00883 BPB, all of it weight quantisation
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight round 37). DeepSeek session. References #820.
Instrument: `geometric-stack lut-evaluate artifact=... model=... valid=... windows=64 reference=true`.

WHY THIS MEASUREMENT WAS NEEDED
  Round 36 established that the served artifact is FREE-transport and that the 0.05522 BPB gap it had
  been credited with was assembled from numbers taken on DIFFERENT position sets (a 512-window float
  evaluation against a 64-window integer evaluation, and a snap-evaluate counterfactual in between).
  An independent review's inference that roughly 0.046 BPB was un-attributed integer serving
  arithmetic rested explicitly on that cross-set arithmetic and asked for this measurement instead.

THE MEASUREMENT (chat-v0-p2/heldout sha256 e5f400b0..., 64 windows, 24,576 targets, one position set)

    float      the model's own f32 kernels                      nll 1.8171591   0.92394 BPB
    reference  its dequantized matrices, grid-code scalars
               and integer biases with unit norm gains, in f32  nll 1.8345347   0.93277 BPB
    integer    the multiplier-free served engine                nll 1.8345360   0.93277 BPB

    quantisation   float -> reference   0.0174 nats   0.00883 BPB
    engine         reference -> integer 0.000001 nats 0.000001 BPB
    total          float -> integer     0.0174 nats   0.00884 BPB
    top-1 agreement between the engine and the float model: 0.9244

WHAT IT SETTLES
  1. THE INTEGER ENGINE IS EXACT. It reproduces its own dequantized reference to one micro-nat
     per token. There is no serving-arithmetic loss to find: not in activation fixed-point, not in
     the tables, not in the norm, not in the l2 distance. The hypothesis that ~0.046 BPB sat in the
     integer path is refuted, and it was an artefact of comparing different position sets.
  2. THE WHOLE SERVED COST IS WEIGHT QUANTISATION, 0.00883 BPB. This matches the round-20
     rounding attribution (0.0173756 nats, 0.0088 BPB) exactly, which is the independent
     confirmation that both tools are measuring the same thing on the same weights.
  3. THE SERVED ARTIFACT DELIVERS 99.0 PER CENT OF ITS OWN FLOAT MODEL, and the earlier "0.05522
     BPB served gap" was a protocol artefact rather than a serving deficit. The published
     decomposition of that gap into snap and quantisation terms is superseded; the real one is one
     line: 0.00883 BPB of quantisation, 0.000001 BPB of engine.
  4. CONSEQUENTLY THERE IS NO LARGE SERVING HEADROOM LEFT. Rounds 24-34 spent their effort on the
     transport snap (0.03578 BPB credited, now known to be a float counterfactual the artifact does
     not pay) and on quantiser arms (GPTQ vs round-to-nearest, 0.00376 BPB). Both were real
     questions about real quantities, and neither was where the served number was being decided.
     What decides it is the MODEL's own quality on the evaluation slice: 0.92394 BPB for its float
     kernels here, against 0.87755 measured on a different 512-window slice in round 16.

THE REMAINING QUESTION IS THEREFORE MODEL QUALITY, NOT SERVING
  The pipeline is faithful: train -> export -> serve costs 0.0088 BPB and the engine adds nothing.
  Every remaining lever is about the model and its data - which is where round 12's measurement
  (in-domain data worth 0.1592 BPB against 0.0837 for size, extended at 10x in round 35 where a
  214 M model at 1.0966 lost to a 7.16 M chat-only model at 0.9607) already pointed.

SCOPE
  One model, one artifact, one corpus, 64 windows, 24,576 targets, single deterministic run. The
  comparison is exactly matched on positions, which is what makes it decisive where the earlier
  cross-set numbers were not.
