UOR-R4 — Where the served gap lives: 65 % transport snap, 35 % quantisation, and calibration does not help
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 16-19). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE QUESTION
  The end-to-end chain serves 0.93277 bits per byte where its own fused float model scores
  0.87755 on the same held-out stream (sha e6f... see the corpus line below). A 0.05522 BPB gap.
  Is it a knob the project can turn, or a property of the served representation?

THE MEASUREMENTS (all on chat-v0-p2/heldout, sha256 e5f400b0..., 512-window float reference and
  24,576-target integer evaluations, byte basis 2.837427 bytes per token)

  stage                                   nll                     bits per byte     cost
  fused float reference                   1.7259248122572899      0.87755            --
  transport-snapped reference             1.7963021881878376      0.91333           +0.03578
  multiplier-free D11, export a1          1.8345359993679817      0.93277           +0.01944
  (GPTQ with calibration - see the round-22 correction; the export method is selected by
   whether calibration tokens are supplied, so this arm is GPTQ, not round-to-nearest)
  (64 calibration windows, damp 0.01)

  total served-to-fused gap                                                          +0.05522
  share from the transport snap                                                        64.8 %

  Second export arm - MORE calibration, FINER damping:
  multiplier-free D11, export a2          1.836327732755273       0.93368           +0.00091
  (256 calibration windows, damp 0.001)                                            versus a1

FINDINGS
  1. Export tuning does not buy fidelity here. Quadrupling calibration windows and tightening
     damping by an order of magnitude makes the served artifact 0.00091 BPB WORSE, not better.
     The evaluation is deterministic (same positions, integer logits, top-1 agreement 1.0 between
     engines), so this is a property of the two artifacts and not sampling noise. The hypothesis
     that calibration is the knob is retired.
  2. The gap is two thirds transport snap and one third quantisation. The snap is not an export
     setting: it is the representation choice that maps continuous transport onto the 120 unit
     icosians, and it costs 0.03578 BPB by itself. A finer quantisation grid can only address the
     smaller 0.01944 BPB term.
  3. Therefore the lever is the representation, not the exporter: if the served gap is to close,
     it closes by making the snapped representation itself richer or by reducing what snapping
     discards, not by exporting more carefully.

REPRODUCTION, MEASURED
  Rn independent retrain of the same 19.9 M stack on a different GPU (5090 -> 4090), a different
  binary commit and a different pod reproduces the multiplier-free served nll to 2.1e-6
  (1.8345338587 -> 1.8345359994) with top-1 agreement 1.0 throughout. The train, export and
  multiplier-free serve chain is deterministic in the quantity that matters. This is the
  execution policy's "do not claim bitwise cross-backend reproducibility without measuring it"
  case, now measured rather than assumed.

OPERATIONAL NOTE
  The identical training recipe ran at 218.6 k tokens per second on an idle pod and 126.9 k with a
  CPU-bound evaluation holding six-plus cores alongside it: a 42 % slowdown. "One job per GPU"
  does not cover a competing job that is not on a GPU at all.

SCOPE
  One model, one corpus, one held-out stream, one byte basis, single seed for training, two export
  configurations. The a2 arm differs from a1 in calibration_windows (64 -> 256) and damp
  (0.01 -> 0.001) only. Bit-level claims are about nll reproduction, not about every intermediate.
