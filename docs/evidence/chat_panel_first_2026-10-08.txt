WHAT THE MODEL IS AND IS NOT
  IS: fluent. It writes multi-clause, correctly punctuated English; it latches onto salient words
  from the prompt ("morning" -> morning routine, "Japan" -> Japan, "Ana" -> Ana); it produced one
  correct factual answer; it volunteers conversational openers ("Yes, I can help you with that").
  IS NOT: responsive. It does not answer instructions, it repeats itself, it decorates replies with
  format artifacts from its training data (markdown headings, "<<Title>>" wrappers, numbered lists),
  and multi-turn state does not drive behaviour - the identical reply to both turns of "My name is
  Ana." / "What is my name?" is the clearest single failure in the panel.

WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT KIND OF RESULT
  This profile is exactly what the measurements predict: a 19.9 M model at 0.93277 bits per byte has
  learned the STYLE of conversation and a little of its content, and has not learned to follow
  instructions. The qualitative failure mode matches the quantitative one, which is what makes the
  number trustworthy and what makes the next lever identifiable.

  It also sharpens the data question. The corpus that produced this is chat-Style-heavy - the model
  imitates the register of answers without binding them to the question. More tokens of the same
  templated sources (which is what rebuilding chat-v1-p2-train would buy) is therefore the WEAKER
  version of the data lever: what this panel shows missing is instruction-to-response binding, not
  volume. A corpus with instruction/response structure, or a training objective that binds the reply
  to the turn, is the better-targeted spend - and the two dose-response arms now running test only
  the volume axis.

SCOPE
  One artifact, one decoding setting (greedy), 11 requests, automatic proxies rather than human or
  model-based judging. No claim is made about what a larger model of the same family would do, and
  none of this is a claim that the architecture is at fault: the same served path reproduced its own
  float model to 0.00883 BPB.
