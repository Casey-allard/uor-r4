UOR-R4 — Diluting the demand mixture keeps compliance but does not restore retention
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 119-122). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE TEST
  Round 119's breakthrough used 2,860 demand-bearing documents (103,169 tokens, about 1.2 % of the
  mixed stream) and produced compliance across every form class while BOTH retention turns dropped to
  "I do". This arm reduces the demand store to 700 documents (25,260 tokens, about 0.03 %) with
  everything else identical: same chat store and hash-proven mask, policy=full_prefix,
  UOR_TERMINAL_WEIGHT=60, the frozen 7.16 M shape, 13,348 steps, panel v2 read as the first two
  content tokens.

RESULT

  form classes under dilution (700 documents)
    one word, colour of grass   "Yellow."        COMPLIES (form right, the fact is not)
    one word, what bees make    "Hydrogen, also"  partial
    yes/no, sky blue            "Yes."           COMPLIES, correct
    yes/no, fish in trees       "No."            COMPLIES, correct
    yes/no, is ice hot          "Yes."           COMPLIES in form only (the fact is wrong)
    number, days in a week      "60."            COMPLIES (form right, the fact is not)
    number, two plus two        "4."             COMPLIES, correct
    number, dog legs            "6."             COMPLIES in form
    list, three colours         "Red, blue,"     COMPLIES (short list)

  retention under dilution
    cats -> "One healthy"                        does NOT answer
    name -> "Imagineopotamids have"              does NOT answer, and degenerates

  So COMPLIANCE SURVIVES A 4x DILUTION OF THE DEMAND STORE, and RETENTION DOES NOT RETURN AT EITHER
  CONCENTRATION. The trade seen in round 119 is therefore NOT a simple mixture-ratio effect at this
  scale: reducing the demand share by 4x leaves retention exactly as broken as it was.

WHAT IT ESTABLISHES
  1. COMPLIANCE IS ROBUST TO THE MIXTURE RATIO. Four form classes comply with a demand store that is
     0.03 % of the training tokens, which is a strong statement about the data-shape explanation: a
     small, well-formed demonstration of demand-and-matching-answer is enough to teach the behaviour.
     That also makes the fix cheap - the store needed is tiny, not a corpus.
  2. RETENTION IS A SEPARATE PROBLEM, NOT A WEIGHT. It held in the full_prefix arms without any
     demand store (both turns answered "My name", round 101's 60x arm) and fails with the store
     present at both concentrations tested. The natural next candidates are the store's CONTENT
     (its documents are single-turn by construction, so the mixture acquires thousands of documents
     that never require carrying anything across a turn) and the terminal weighting interacting with
     multi-turn documents. Both are testable: a demand store built with TWO-turn documents whose
     second turn demands a form would separate them, and it is a small extension of the generator
     that already exists.
  3. It also confirms the qualitative picture from round 119 with a weaker dose: the model now answers
     "Yellow.", "Yes.", "No.", "4.", "60.", "Red, blue," where every arm before round 119 produced
     prose for all of these.

WHAT REMAINS, NAMED
  * Restore retention while keeping compliance: build a demand store whose documents are TWO-turn -
    a first turn that states something and a second that demands a form about it - so the mixture no
    longer trades multi-turn conditioning away for form compliance.
  * Factual accuracy inside the correct form is still imperfect at this size ("Yellow" for grass,
    "60" for the days in a week), which is a separate and much more familiar scaling question now
    that the form itself is solved.

SCOPE
  Two concentrations of one demand store, one mixture, one weight, one shape, single seed each, greedy
  decoding, one panel. Compliance is measured on nine form-demanded turns and retention on two pairs;
  the counts are exact for this panel and are not a benchmark.
