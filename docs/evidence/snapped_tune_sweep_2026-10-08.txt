UOR-R4 — The snapped-training route is closed for this quantiser, and the damage is a basin effect, not a dose
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight round 33). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE SWEEP
  Same 19.9 M chat stack, same held-out stream (chat-v0-p2/heldout, sha256 e5f400b0...), same
  evaluation (snap-evaluate 512 windows for the float side; export with GPTQ at the measured
  optimum, then d11-evaluate on 24,576 targets for the served side).

  arm        snapped float   served (D11)   quantisation cost
  no tune    0.91333         0.93277        0.01944 BPB  (0.0382 nats)
  250-step   0.89420         0.95540        0.06117 BPB  (0.1203 nats)
  500-step   0.89113         0.95150        0.06033 BPB  (0.1187 nats)

FINDINGS
  1. BOTH tuned arms serve WORSE than no tune: +0.02263 BPB for 250 steps and +0.01873 for 500,
     against a float-side improvement in both cases. The float gain does not survive the artifact.
  2. THE DAMAGE IS NOT DOSE-DEPENDENT. Quantisation cost jumps from 0.01944 to 0.06117 BPB on
     entering the snapped basin with 250 steps, and then essentially plateaus: 500 steps costs
     0.06033, slightly LESS than 250. Twice the tune is not twice the damage; the first snapped
     steps do the damage and further steps do not add to it.
  3. Therefore the mechanism is a BASIN effect, not a length effect. Snapped fine-tuning moves the
     weights to a region of parameter space that this quantiser serves poorly; once there, staying
     longer neither helps nor hurts the servability much. A shorter tune is not the fix, which is
     what the sweep was run to test.
  4. The served ordering is not the float ordering. By float, 500 < 250 < no tune. By served,
     no tune < 500 < 250. Anyone selecting on the float proxy would pick exactly the wrong arm.

WHAT IT CLOSES, AND WHAT IT LEAVES OPEN
  CLOSED: recovering the 0.03578 BPB transport-snap term by training in the snapped representation
  and exporting with GPTQ. The route costs more in servability than it returns in fidelity, at
  every length tested, and the cost appears at the first snapped step.
  OPEN, and now better aimed: the problem is that the quantiser does not tolerate the snapped
  basin. Two routes remain - a quantiser or bit allocation that tolerates it (the round-20
  attribution says the weight matrices carry the loss, but the tool refuses snapped artifacts, so
  this needs a measurement that does not exist yet), or serving the snap differently rather than
  training into it. Neither is a sweep and neither is claimed here.

  The best served result on real held-out chat remains the untuned artifact at 0.93277 BPB,
  4.70 times better than the 4.3885 this project serves today.

SCOPE
  One model, one corpus, one stream, two tune lengths, single seed each, one quantiser setting.
  The three arms differ in the number of fine-tune steps and nothing else that is recorded.
