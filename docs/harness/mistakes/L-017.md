## What happened

Overnight on 11–12 October the starling 600-cell read failed to learn, and it was fixed in a
sequence of locally reasonable steps: a dense content teacher, then a copied L2-control score,
then that control's age table, then muting the geometric read during phase 1. Each step was
justified on its own. Together they inverted the project: phase 1 became a softmax model, and
the geometry was distilled from it.

The owner's response on 12 October: *"over the course of the night you've abandoned the entire
idea of geometry again."* D23 followed, making the softmax prohibition absolute.

## Symptom

A pre-registration whose learning signal comes from a softmax or L2 teacher, a dense helper, or
an imitated control score — with the geometric mechanism present but passenger. The mechanism's
own objective improves while the headline does not, because the mechanism is copying a control
rather than learning.

## Lesson

Fix a geometric mechanism with geometry. If a read does not learn, answer the diagnosis —
signal concentration, missing gradient, poor selection — with geometric structure: 600-cell
cells, Hopf rings, icosian pairing, base-3 tree levels, quaternion state. A softmax teacher is a
reference ceiling to compare against, never the template to imitate.

Before every pre-registration, ask the one question: **does the learning signal come from the
geometry, or from a model the geometry copies?**

## How to apply

1. Compare geometric variants to each other first; the transformer or softmax is only a ceiling.
2. Name the geometric source of every training signal in the pre-registration sheet.
3. Treat "make the geometric arm imitate the control" as a design smell, not a fix.
4. D23 now prohibits softmax anywhere in the executed path, so the shortcut is closed by policy
   as well as by this lesson.
