## What happened

On 2026-10-03 a cleanup killed processes matched by `cargo (fmt|check|test)`. That terminated
the Codex lab's focused integer test with exit 143, and Codex had to report the loss on #1552.
Earlier `pkill -f` patterns had also matched the author's own SSH sessions.

## Symptom

A lab reports that its build or test died with exit 143 or simply vanished, at a moment when
another lab was cleaning up. The victim has no record of stopping it.

## Lesson

The laptop is shared. A process name is not ownership. Terminate only PIDs your own launcher
recorded — write `$!` to a pidfile at launch, or select by your unique `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, out
root or worktree. Before any kill, list the candidates with `ps -o pid,args` and confirm each
one is yours, then use `kill <pid>`, never a pattern.

Related: [working-in-one-checkout.md](../../labs/working-in-one-checkout.md), which puts every
lab in one checkout — the shared-resource problem this lesson is about.

## How to apply

1. Launch long jobs so they are identifiable: a pidfile, a unique target dir, or a unique out root.
2. To stop one, `kill $(cat pidfile)`. Not `pkill -f cargo`. Not `pgrep -f rustc`.
3. If a job cannot be identified, leave it alone and ask whose it is.
