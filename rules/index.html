UOR-R4

The rules of the project, in plain language

UOR-R4 is trying to build a fast language model that runs on an ordinary laptop and does not use the expensive machinery inside today's chat AIs. A short rulebook, the charter, keeps every contributor pointed at that goal. This page explains what the charter says, why each rule exists, what the project's labs may and may not do, and who makes which decision. It is a guide to the charter, not a replacement: if the two ever disagree, the charter wins.

flowchart TD
  idea["Idea or experiment"] --> rules{"Inside the constraints?<br/>Geometry only, no squashing,<br/>no matmul"}
  rules -- "No" --> owner["Only the owner can<br/>change the charter"]
  rules -- "Yes" --> need{"Needs GPU training or evaluation,<br/>or will a milestone claim rest on it?"}
  need -- "No: builds, kernel tests,<br/>host checks" --> doit["Just do it"]
  need -- "Yes" --> sheet["Pre-registration: one-page sheet<br/>bar, noise, controls, seeds,<br/>cost, what each outcome decides"]
  sheet --> fit{"FIT review by someone<br/>other than the author"}
  fit -- "Not fit" --> sheet
  fit -- "FIT" --> release["Lab D67 pre-run review:<br/>whole project, tools, inputs,<br/>FIT and lease checked"]
  release --> run["Run on the GPU via uor-pod"]
  run --> row["Notebook row: date, what ran,<br/>number, bar, verdict, artifact"]
  doit --> row

How work moves from an idea to a recorded result. Only the Yes branch that needs GPU spend or a milestone claim goes through a sheet, a FIT review and the lab's pre-run review.

Why there are rules at all

The owner's stated goal behind every ban, in his own words from 18 October 2026, is "to ban expensive operations" and to optimise for "fast local ai that can train fast and run fast" and reaches "almost frontier level capability" (CHARTER.md §1, from decision D37). Each rule removes a cost that a dense neural network pays, or protects the evidence that tells the project whether its geometric replacement works. When a case is unclear, the charter says to choose what trains and serves fastest on a laptop through the geometric path, and to write the choice down.

The project's headline metric is tokens per second: how fast the model reads and writes text, with decode speed and context speed measured separately. A small quality gap is acceptable (CHARTER.md §1). The project is pre-alpha, 1 of its 8 milestones is complete, and no model holds a useful conversation yet (PLAN and rendered STATE; see the mechanism board).

These are rules, so the labels "proof", "measurement" and "hypothesis" from docs/formal_vocabulary.md do not apply to them. The "why" given for each rule below is the reason the charter itself states. It is not a measured result.

The constraints and the reason for each

The charter's section 3 lists thirteen technical constraints. Here they are in everyday terms. Terms are defined in the glossary.

#RuleWhat it means in practiceWhy the charter gives
3.1Geometry onlyEvery mechanism, training signal and fix comes from the project's geometry (prime addresses, rotations, E8/H4 structures). The flock rule for attention looks at about 7 nearest neighbours, not a dense teacher.The bet is that geometry replaces expensive dense operations.
3.2No squashing functionsNo softmax, log-softmax, cross-entropy, softplus, sigmoid, tanh or GELU anywhere, including in losses, sampling and comparators, and including renamed or lookup-table versions.They are expensive and hide a dense global normalisation.
3.3No matrix multiplicationNo dense matmul in training, evaluation, tooling or serving, including wrappers and hand-written loops. A geometric feed-forward block is allowed; a dense MLP is not.Matmul is the cost the project exists to remove.
3.4Presence countsBanned code may not sit in any compiled or loaded module. NVRTC (runtime GPU compilation) is banned. The checks uor-harness ratchet d23 and d25 only ever tighten.Code that is present eventually runs.
3.5Research compute is GPU-firstGPU results qualify training, evaluation and reference runs by default, and GPU is spent only where a run can change a decision. D84 permits only the named owner-authorized CPU training program to run development training, evaluation and parity in its isolated module; those results cannot replace a GPU bar.Host runs historically gave wrong timing and evidence, while a bounded CPU port can repair lawful training paths on the owner's workstation without becoming qualification evidence.
3.6Serving is integer, on your deviceThe shipped model uses no float or transcendental instruction. Since D80 and D81 (28 Oct 2026) it may use the hardware integer multiply and divide, which give exact products and quotients. Running it on a laptop CPU, in a browser (WebAssembly) or on Apple silicon is allowed, and so is measuring its speed and energy. Panel scoring, qualification and milestone evaluation of an exported artifact remain GPU research compute; only product serving and measuring its tokens/s and energy run on client devices.The product is fast local AI on ordinary hardware.
3.7Float only offline, with an export gapFloat training is legal offline in a separately scanned module. Every float candidate reports how much accuracy it loses when exported to integers; a loss above the two-seed noise band disqualifies it.Float trains faster, but only the integer artifact ships.
3.8Zeta exceptionFloat is allowed for complex-plane zeta-zero parts and prime log-phases. Everything downstream is discrete.Zeta phases are complex numbers by nature.
3.9Efficiency is measuredReport sparse access, bytes per token, bounded candidates and exact memory together.Unmeasured efficiency is not efficiency.
3.10No neural comparisonsNo neural comparators, controls, teachers or graders until the owner says the model works end to end. Panels are graded by deterministic checks. The one exception is a one-time donor pass (D41) with binding conditions.A neural comparator would hide a dense model behind the geometry.
3.11Rust firstNew code is Rust, built on the uor-foundation crates. Python only where Rust cannot do the job.One fast, checked language keeps training and serving on one path.
3.12Exact identity stays separate from addressOne shared vector writer serves input, carry and output. Every artifact binds its data, seed, configuration and parameters.One representation avoids translation costs, and exact identity cannot be recovered from a geometric address.
3.13HygieneTyped errors instead of panics, no committed binaries, corpora or secrets, and API keys are never read or printed.It keeps the repository small and the keys out of it.

Source: CHARTER.md §3; the first column gives its clause numbers.

What labs may do, and what they may not

A lab is any contributor, human or AI, working under the charter. The rules in sections 3 and 5 give these limits.

Labs may:

  • Choose their own work from PLAN.md, post a one-line intent note, claim a branch and carry on unless redirected (CHARTER.md §5.3).
  • Build, run finite kernel tests and run host checks without any pre-registration (§4.5).
  • Train in float offline with a sheet, a non-author FIT and the lab's own whole-project pre-run review under D67, and report the export gap on the same evaluator (§3.7, §4.5, §5.1).
  • Run the named D84 CPU training and parity program as development research without treating its output as a GPU qualification result (§3.5).
  • Serve the integer model on a user's device and measure it (§3.6).
  • Review their own pull request at the exact head, from a fresh context (§5.5), and use a fresh-context subagent as the non-author FIT reviewer (§4.5).

Labs may not:

  • Use a banned operation anywhere, including in comparators, or in renamed form (§3.2, §3.3).
  • Run CPU model compute outside the named D84 program, or spend GPU on work that cannot change a decision (§3.5).
  • Start GPU training or a milestone-bearing run without a sheet, a non-author FIT and the lab's own pre-run review (§4.5, §5.1). D41's donor pass is the named exception that also needs the owner at dispatch.
  • Use a neural comparator, teacher or grader, apart from the single D41 donor pass (§3.10).
  • Cancel a healthy run because a self-set budget ran out, or touch another session's GPU or files (§5.4).
  • Stop a mechanism. That needs the owner (§4.1).
  • Delete research. Negatives, unique source and other labs' work go to the archive unchanged (§4.7).
  • Exceed 4 pods or $8 per hour across all labs (§5.4).

How evidence is judged

Section 4 of the charter is about not fooling ourselves.

  • A negative closes one configuration, never a mechanism. Mechanisms carry one of three statuses: ACTIVE (on the model path), READY (built and tested, not yet trained in) or PARKED (a tested form failed or broke a rule; the reopen conditions are listed). There is no "retired" status for a mechanism (§4.1). Rules can be retired; mechanisms cannot.
  • Instrument before verdict. If a check fails, first ask whether the measuring tool or the mechanism is at fault. A broken instrument is a fix, not a negative (§4.2).
  • Power. A decisive run uses at least 2 seeds or at least 200 fresh rows, with bars no narrower than the measured noise, and reports a fresh transfer number with every development gain (§4.3).
  • Controls and pivots. Compare against lawful non-neural baselines such as count tables. After three results on one line that do not move its headline number, write a pivot note: stop, or run one decisive experiment (§4.4).
  • Claims. Write "measured X on Y against Z". A test pass is not trained capability, and NOT_RUN is not a pass (§4.6).
  • The first-milestone closing bar (original text preserved). For milestone M1, the integer model's top-1 accuracy through the D11 export must beat both the unigram table (1,226 of 14,287) and the T2 successor table (6,300 of 14,287) on the same development panel, on each of two seeds, with tokens per second reported (§4.8).
  • Dated close outcome, not a new bar in this PR. Decision log 6515151261 records the pre-existing D68 whole-project lab decision approving M1's close and the supersession of the older criteria. The original D39 acceptance text and upstream evidence remain linked in the M1 item and dated records; this documentation does not rewrite them.
