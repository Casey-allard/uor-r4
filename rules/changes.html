Change log for the project's rules

This is the dated record of every change to the project's rulebook, the charter. Only the owner changes the charter, and every change is written here as a new entry, newest first. If you want to know why a rule is the way it is, or when it last moved, start here. The log began on 19 October 2026 with the charter's adoption.

flowchart LR
  a["Old rules<br/>D0 to D41, AGENTS.md"] -- "19 Oct 2026<br/>checkpoint 1" --> b["CHARTER.md<br/>adopted"]
  b -- "owner changes only,<br/>one dated entry each" --> c["Next entry<br/>in this log"]

The charter replaced the older rules in one step on 19 October 2026. Later changes are added to this log as dated entries.

How an entry is written

An entry gives the date, what changed, which charter clauses it touches, and who decided. The owner's own words are recorded verbatim when they are the reason. Old text is never edited or deleted: it moves unchanged to the archive/ folder, and the live decision index keeps one line saying where each decision went (see the decision map).

24 October 2026: the single-lab rule (D65–D71)

Decided by: the owner (Casey), in Claude's chat, after Claude's review of why the loop stalled. From 23 Oct 14:10Z to 24 Oct 13:43Z the labs merged 70 PRs and closed no issue. Then every lab stopped behind asks nobody answered, and when the owner restarted the laptop at 13:58Z nothing restarted the labs. Claude asked four design questions, each with options worded by Claude. The owner answered:

  • "One queue (Recommended)";
  • "Yes, where headless (Recommended)";
  • "The finishing PR (Recommended)";
  • on held work, in his own words: "i never see these requests for released, so I would say do not park any work at all for any reason - if there was only one single lab running what would the process be? a single lab can't park and wait for another lab or me to review. It must be able to accurately judge whether or not, with an advesareal review, whether or not the GPU run is the correct test at that time, and whether the correct tools exist in either the repo or the huggingface repo that will make them successful. We don't want to be doing runs if they are not going to unlock further work, like when we finish a mechanism and are simply waiting on the gpu training or something to happen which unlocks further work, we want to do those as highest priority. It's all about removing blockers at this point."

Claude read the rule back, including that the current holds become runnable through a pre-run review within the caps; the owner answered "Accurate (Recommended)". Asked what should still wait for him, he wrote: "in theory the project structure should be build in the github issues so there should not be a point where anything is waiting on my decision - make sure before the decisions are made that the entire project is understood and that we understand from a whole project context what this step is for and what it is achieving and that it makes sense to do it - do not approve without larger context". Claude's read-back had kept a 30-minute wait for another lab's review; the owner rejected it: "Reviewers waiting 30 minutes for other labs to see it is the entire problem. Posting and waiting for an answer is where we lose." He then approved this record: "Yes, start phase 0: open the records PR with my words verbatim (single-lab rule, review inside the unit, pre-run and decision reviews, post-merge audits, owner queue as a log) and post the #2028 note with each lab's first move. I'll reopen the labs."

  1. The single-lab rule (D65). Every step is one a single lab can finish alone. Other labs and the owner speed work up or reverse it, never hold it, and no step posts a question and waits for the answer. An unanswered PM ask becomes the asking lab's own decision review. (Clauses 5.1, 5.2, 5.10.)
  2. Review inside the unit; audits after the merge (D66). The lab's own fresh-context adversarial review is the exact-head review, and the PR queues in the same session. Other labs audit merged work and file repairs. (Clause 5.5.)
  3. The pre-run review replaces the owner release (D67). A GPU or training run, held work included, goes ahead when the lab's own pre-run review passes, inside the caps, highest unblock value first. (Clauses 5.1, 5.10.)
  4. The decision review replaces decision cards to the PM or owner (D68). Each review reads the whole project before it approves. (Clauses 5.1, 5.2.)
  5. The owner's queue is a log (D69). The owner sets only the charter, §3 with its exceptions, and the GPU caps; everything done on his behalf is logged and reversible. (Clause 5.1.)
  6. The finishing PR closes its issue (D70) (clause 5.5), and a supervisor restarts idle labs (D71) (clause 5.10).

19 October 2026: charter adopted at checkpoint 1

Decided by: the owner (Casey), in chat, at checkpoint 1 of the repository renormalisation. The charter replaces the stacked directions in AGENTS.md and decisions D0 to D41 as the project's rules (decision D42). Source: CHARTER.md, the final DECISIONS.md index, and the checkpoint notes in charter-diff-notes.md.

The owner answered eight questions. One line each, with the clause it changed:

  1. Client serving (Q1). D24's ban on CPU compute governs research compute only. Serving the exported D11 integer model on client devices (CPU, browser WebAssembly, Apple silicon), including measuring tokens per second and energy, is allowed. D36's "browser counts as CPU model compute" clause is replaced. (Clauses 3.5 and 3.6.) Panel scoring, qualification and milestone evaluation of an exported artifact remain GPU research compute; only product serving and measuring its tokens/s and energy run on client devices.
  2. Pre-registration (Q2). A one-page sheet with a non-author FIT review is needed only before GPU training or evaluation, and before any run a milestone claim rests on. A fresh-context subagent counts as non-author. Builds, finite kernel tests and host checks need none. (Clause 4.5.)
  3. Releases (Q3). One typed owner release per pre-registered sheet, covering its arms, seeds and stop rules. D40 is unchanged: Casey types it personally. (Clause 5.1.)
  4. Records (Q4). One notebook page per research line, with one row per result. Dated evidence folders exist only for sealed artifacts. NOW.md plus the notebooks replace current-state.md, and STATUS and ROADMAP change only with state. (Clause 5.6.)
  5. Coordination (Q5). The owner plus one project-manager seat (Kimi, with a cover lab named by the owner). Labs self-direct from NOW.md. The D14 three-seat council is retired, and operating notes may not contradict the charter. (Clauses 5.2 and 5.3.)
  6. First-milestone bar (Q6). For M1, bar (d) is replaced: integer top-1 through the D11 export must beat both the unigram table (1,226 of 14,287) and the T2 successor table (6,300 of 14,287) on the same development panel, on each of two seeds, with tokens per second reported. BPB (bits per byte) returns later as a bar once a lawful likelihood exists. Bars (a), (b), (c) and (e) are unchanged. (Clause 4.8.)
  7. Harness (Q7). The harness stays as a shared view with automatic presence and no manual heartbeat cadence. The merge queue serialises itself. The adopt_policy hold is dropped, and claims record the charter version. Discretionary harness features are frozen until a model beats both count tables; mandated Rust ports (D27) continue. (Clauses 5.3 and 5.5.)
  8. Legacy crates (Q8). The four older crates (uor-r4-graph-compiler, uor-r4-graph-certify, uor-r4-api, uor-r4-proof-model) and the root crate are repaired on the current core instead of archived. Repair restores the build only; their mechanisms stay PARKED. (Clause 2.4.) The draft had recommended archiving them; the owner chose repair.

Approved in the same session: archive everything byte-identical with nothing deleted; side projects under projects/; documentation as GitHub markdown plus an mdBook site with the Studio at /studio; the web client as a local in-browser WebAssembly integer model; the desktop client as a Tauri app (specification only); and D22 kept, so mechanism statuses are ACTIVE, READY and PARKED with no terminal state. Source: CHARTER.md and decision D42 in docs/integration/DECISIONS.md.
