## The mistake and the contract

The writer stores a same-value assertion over a live head as a nonconflicting record with `action` one and a nonzero `previous` link; a differing assertion is marked a conflict. The version-intent scan followed only explicit revisions (`action` two), and `e44a9ae4` treated any failure to reach a genuine root as proof that the original was absent. That confused traversal rejection with physical absence: all three records of the reassertion chain were resident and the live directory named the head.

The repaired contract, versioned in the witness as `reassertion_links`, has two parts. A validated descending link is either an explicit revision or a resident same-owner, same-value, nonconflicting reassertion of a strictly older record; the value equality is re-proven byte for byte against the resident predecessor rather than trusted from the conflict flag. A chain is truncated only when the record where the walk stopped names a strictly older predecessor whose ring slot now holds a different identity; a conflicting, foreign-owner, malformed or otherwise rejected link is not evidence of eviction and yields no abstention candidate. Depth counts validated record links, not distinct values, so a reassertion adds one link; the class features stay chain positions. The frozen immediate-previous reader is unchanged: the previous version of a revised head is still the record it names, which after a reassertion is the reassertion itself.

The same contract is carried through selection, the field-anchor proof, emission and checkpoint restore: an ancestor anchor records `reassertion_links` and restores only under exactly that contract, while a legacy anchor claiming a revision-only proof for a path with a reassertion link is rejected, as is a reassertion whose value no longer matches. Legacy artifacts have neither flag on the wire and keep their exact behavior; `e3a906c8` and `e44a9ae4` are unchanged files with unchanged outputs under this source. A `HistoricalVersionIntentReassertionDisabled` control withholds the reassertion links and the eviction proof, exposing the legacy contract for causal comparison. A head that is itself a same-value reassertion still has no historical read, as under the frozen parent; this is a stated limit, not a claim.

## Limitations and next implementation

Abstention after a reassertion chain loses its root is a decision by exact-record identity: the surviving reassertion carries the same value as the evicted original, yet it is not the initial record, so the model answers `Unknown.` rather than recovering the value; this is the authored expectation, frozen before measurement, and a design choice that a later cycle may revisit explicitly. A head that is itself a same-value reassertion has no historical read under the frozen parent contract and preserves the parent's answer. Plain-style initial requests under two phrasings still receive the inherited owner sentence for the same exact record; exact authored text alone is reported separately. Depth counts validated record links, so a request for an intermediate version is neither labeled nor claimed. The review prompts became open development evidence in this cycle; the independent draw is the only held-out gate. These are bounded authored forms with fresh spellings, not temporal language understanding.

Next under #973, once this repair is qualified: intermediate-version intent with exact depth labels, so a request for a specific earlier version selects that exact record or abstains when the chain no longer holds it, while preserving initial, previous and current behavior, reassertions, competing owners, dependency abstention and causal follow-ups.
