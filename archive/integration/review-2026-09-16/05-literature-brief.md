# F. Literature Brief: What the Field Knows That a No-MatMul Geometric LM Team Must Know

**Prepared:** 16 September 2026
**Scope:** Local LM on M1-class laptops; no transformer backbone; no mathematical matrix multiplication at inference (offline training may use floats/matmul); exact addressed memory; finite group/geometric state (quaternions, H4 / 600-cell root system, Hamming codes, XOR accumulators); prime-based identity; fixed zeta-zero phase features; learned lookup tables and finite typed operators (Read/Copy/Add/Emit). Long-term target: "frontier-quality" prose, memory, reasoning, coding.

**Verification protocol.** Every paper cited below was resolved to a real record through the alphaXiv discover/content tools, the Firecrawl research index, Exa web search, or the `export.arxiv.org` Atom API (title, author list, date checked on 2026-09-16). Where a *number* comes from a paper's abstract or a tool-returned structured report, it is stated plainly. Where I rely on my own recollection of a paper body that I did not re-open in this session, it is tagged **[BK]** (background knowledge) and should be treated as "probably right, re-check before quoting." Web sources (blogs, GitHub READMEs, benchmark repos) are tagged **[web]** and are lower-tier evidence than peer-reviewed or arXiv papers. Nothing here is fabricated; where the literature is empty, I say so.

## A. MatMul-free, ternary, and lookup-table inference

### A.1 What has actually been demonstrated

**BitNet b1.58 (Ma et al., 2024).** "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits" (arXiv:2402.17764) trained transformers with ternary weights {-1,0,+1} and 8-bit activations (W1.58A8) from scratch and reported that at 3B parameters the ternary model matched a full-precision LLaMA in perplexity and zero-shot accuracy while being roughly 2.7x faster and using roughly 3.5x less GPU memory; the paper's headline energy figure (about 71x lower *arithmetic* energy for the matmul component at 70B) is a first-principles operator-count estimate, not a wall-socket measurement **[BK]**. The predecessor "BitNet: Scaling 1-bit Transformers for Large Language Models" (Wang et al., 2023, arXiv:2310.11453) established the BitLinear + straight-through-estimator recipe.

**BitNet b1.58 2B4T (Microsoft, 2025).** The technical report (arXiv:2504.12285) is the first open-weights *native* ternary model at 2B parameters, trained on 4T tokens. Reported numbers (from the report): non-embedding memory 0.4 GB vs. 1.4-4.8 GB for comparable FP16 models; CPU time-per-output-token 29 ms (vs. 124 ms for MiniCPM 2B); *estimated* energy 0.028 J/token (vs. 0.186-0.649 J for the comparison set; again an arithmetic-operations estimate). Quality: average 54.19 on their benchmark suite vs. Qwen2.5-1.5B at 55.23; best-in-class on ARC-C (49.91), PIQA (77.09), WinoGrande (71.90), GSM8K (58.38); MMLU 53.17. It beats INT4 post-training-quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B and beats 7-8B models that were PTQ-ed to 1.58 bits. Two facts matter for this team: (i) the architecture is still a *transformer* with RoPE, attention (Q-K dot products in higher precision), and ternary linear layers; (ii) it took 4T tokens and Microsoft-scale compute to reach parity with a 1.5B FP16 model.

**BitNet v2 (2025, arXiv:2504.18415)** pushes activations to native 4-bit via a Hadamard transform; **BitNet Distillation (2025, arXiv:2510.13998)** fine-tunes off-the-shelf FP models (e.g., Qwen) into 1.58-bit; **Sparse-BitNet (2026, arXiv:2603.05168)** combines N:M sparsity with ternary weights; **"Breaking the 1.58-bit Barrier" (Intel, Sept 2026, arXiv:2609.16338)** measures 29 ternary models, finds up to 51.5% zeros, and proposes a bitmap+sign layout costing 2-z bits/weight (as low as 1.485 bpw), yielding up to 1.28x kernel speedup and 1.18-1.27x end-to-end decode gains.

**bitnet.cpp / "1-bit AI Infra Part 1.1" (Wang et al., 2024, arXiv:2410.16144).** Lossless ternary kernels for CPUs: 1.37-5.07x speedup on ARM (Apple M2 Ultra used for ARM tests) with 55.4-70.0% energy reduction; 2.37-6.17x on x86 with 71.9-82.2% energy reduction; a 100B ternary model decodes at 5-7 tok/s on a single CPU. (Energy here is measured, not estimated.) [web: github.com/microsoft/BitNet confirms these figures and adds 2026 BitNet-embedding releases.]

**T-MAC (Wei et al., 2024, arXiv:2407.00088).** This is the single most relevant systems paper for a LUT-centric team. T-MAC replaces mixed-precision GEMM (low-bit weights x 8/16-bit activations) with *bit-serial table lookup*: precompute, for each group of activations, the partial sums for all bit patterns; then each weight bit-plane indexes the table. Results: up to 4x throughput and 60-70% energy reduction over llama.cpp; kernel speedups up to 6.6x (avg 3.6x); BitNet-b1.58-3B at 30 tok/s single-core / 71 tok/s eight-core on M2 Ultra and 11 tok/s on Raspberry Pi 5; cost scales linearly with weight bit-width. The lesson is not "LUTs beat MACs" in the abstract; it is that on CPUs, **LUT-based accumulation wins when weights are <=4 bits because it eliminates dequantization and multiplications while keeping additions**.

**ShiftAddLLM (You et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.05981).** Post-training reparameterization of pretrained LLMs into binary weight matrices with group scales, so multiplies become shifts and adds (with LUT-based queries for the binary-matrix products). Reported: average perplexity improvements of 5.6 and 22.7 points vs. the best 3-bit and 2-bit quantized baselines, and >80% memory and energy reductions vs. the original FP models, across five LLM families.

**"Scalable MatMul-free Language Modeling" (Zhu et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.02528).** The closest published cousin to this team's goal. What it removes: all dense matmuls (ternary BitLinear everywhere, including the LM head) and *attention itself* (replaced by a MatMul-free Linear GRU, "MLGRU", trained with parallel scan); what remains: ternary accumulation (adds/subtracts), element-wise (Hadamard) products in gating, RMSNorm with a fixed-point inverse-sqrt, sigmoid via interpolated LUT for Loihi 2. Results: models to 2.7B parameters are competitive with Transformer++ on zero-shot tasks (2.7B wins ARC-C and OpenbookQA); the extrapolated scaling law crosses Transformer++ around 10^23 FLOPs (an extrapolation, not an observation); fused kernel gives 25.6% training speedup and 61% memory reduction at 1.3B; on Intel Loihi 2 the 370M model runs at 59.4 tok/s at 70.8 mJ/token system-wide (single chip 71.3 tok/s at 59 mJ/token, 4.2 W). Important caveat: attempts to ternarize Q and K in attention failed to converge, which is *why* they replaced attention with a recurrence.

**A necessary clarification on the word "MatMul-free".** In every paper above, y = xW with W in {-1,0,+1} is still, mathematically, a matrix-vector product over the integers; what is removed is the *multiplier circuit*, not the linear map. If this team's ban is on the mathematical operation (bilinear maps of learned dense weight matrices) rather than on multipliers, then BitNet, MatMul-free LM, T-MAC and ShiftAddLLM are all *outside* the allowed design space and do not constitute precedent for the team's approach; they constitute precedent only for "LUT/accumulate kernels are fast on CPUs." Conversely, if the ban is on multiplier hardware, all of these are prior art and the team must beat them, not merely differ from them.

### A.2 2025-2026 follow-ups (hardware side)

TOM, a ternary read-only-memory accelerator (2026, arXiv:2602.20662); LUT-based accelerator generation for 1.58-bit inference (2026, arXiv:2604.25183); unified LUT inference with signed-digit K/V caches for ternary LLMs (2026, arXiv:2608.03229); VitaLLM mixed-precision edge accelerator (2026, arXiv:2605.00320); FairyFuse fused ternary CPU kernels (2026, arXiv:2604.20913). I did not open these beyond their abstracts; the trend they collectively confirm is that the community is converging on *ternary-weight transformers plus LUT kernels* as the edge-inference endpoint, with the K/V cache and attention scoring as the last non-ternary component.

### A.3 Lesson for this team

The bar for "no-matmul inference" was already set in mid-2024 at 2.7B parameters with Transformer++-level zero-shot accuracy, and in 2025 at 2B/4T tokens with Qwen2.5-1.5B-level quality. Any claim the team makes must be benchmarked against BitNet b1.58 2B4T (open weights, bitnet.cpp on the same M1) on the same held-out perplexity and the same downstream suite. Energy claims must be *measured* (powermetrics on macOS) rather than operator-counted; both BitNet reports use estimates for energy and measurements only for latency.

## B. Non-transformer sequence models

**Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023, arXiv:2312.00752)** and **Mamba-2 / "Transformers are SSMs" (Dao & Gu, 2024, arXiv:2405.21060)**: selective state-space models with linear-time training via scan; Mamba-3B roughly matched transformers of twice its size on standard zero-shot suites **[BK]**; Mamba-2 exposes the SSM/attention duality (structured state-space duality) and is 2-8x faster than Mamba-1 kernels **[BK]**. **Mamba-3 (Lahoti et al., 2026, arXiv:2603.15569)** refines the recurrence with state-space principles for inference-time compute scaling.

**RWKV-7 "Goose" (Peng et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.14456)**: constant memory and constant time per token; the 2.9B model is reported as 3B SoTA on multilingual and matches 3B SoTA on English downstream tasks, trained on fewer tokens than competitors (a 3.1T-token multilingual corpus is released). Theoretical claim from the abstract: RWKV-7 can perform state tracking and recognize all regular languages, exceeding TC0-bounded transformers under standard conjectures. Predecessors: Eagle/Finch (RWKV-5/6, arXiv:2404.05892). Instability under extreme context is documented in SANE (2026, arXiv:2608.22354).

**xLSTM (Beck et al., 2024, arXiv:2405.04517)** and **xLSTM Scaling Laws (Beck et al., 2025, arXiv:2510.02228)**: IsoFLOP and parametric fits over 80M-7B parameters and 2B-2T tokens report that xLSTM Pareto-dominates transformers on cross-entropy per compute, with the advantage growing with context length. **"On Subquadratic Architectures" (Hartl et al., 2026, arXiv:2606.12364)** compares xLSTM, Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet on code pretraining, distillation and time-series, finding xLSTM strongest and attributing it to state-tracking/memory-correction dynamics. **"Effective Distillation to Hybrid xLSTM Architectures" (2026, arXiv:2603.15590)** documents that distilled linearized models still lag.

**Hyena (Poli et al., 2023, arXiv:2302.10866)**: long implicit convolutions + gating; matched attention quality on The Pile with ~20% less training compute at 2K context and is much faster at 64K **[BK]**. **Based (Arora et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.18668)**: identifies the *state-size vs. recall* tradeoff; fixed-state models (H3, Mamba, RWKV) struggle at in-context recall; Based (linear + sliding-window attention) matches Mamba perplexity at 1.3B and beats it by 6.22 points on recall-intensive tasks; 24x generation throughput vs. FlashAttention-2 for 1.3B/1024 tokens.

**Titans (Behrouz et al., 2024/25, arXiv:2501.00663)**: a neural long-term memory module updated at test time by gradient steps ("surprise"), scaled beyond 2M tokens in needle tasks **[BK]**. **TTT layers (Sun et al., 2024, arXiv:2407.04620)**: the hidden state *is* a small model updated by self-supervised gradient steps per token; TTT-Linear/TTT-MLP match or beat Mamba at 125M-1.3B with better long-context scaling **[BK]**.

**Hierarchical Reasoning Model (Wang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.21734)** and its critique. HRM (27M parameters, ~1000 training examples) claimed ~40% on ARC-AGI-1 public eval. The ARC Prize Foundation's independent verification [web: arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis, 15 Aug 2025; code at github.com/arcprize/hierarchical-reasoning-model-analysis] found: verified 32% on the ARC-AGI-1 semi-private set ($1.48/task) and 2% on ARC-AGI-2; (1) the *hierarchical* H/L architecture had minimal impact versus a same-size transformer with the same outer loop; (2) the under-documented *outer refinement loop* drove performance (+13pp from 1 to 2 loops; doubling from 1 to 8), and training with refinement mattered more than inference-time refinement; (3) cross-task transfer was limited: most performance came from memorizing the specific evaluation tasks via a `puzzle_id` embedding (the model cannot be applied to puzzles whose id it has not seen), i.e., it is a transductive, test-time-training regime; (4) 300 augmentations sufficed. The ARC Prize authors hypothesize the purely transductive approach "will not generalize."

**Tiny Recursive Model (Jolicoeur-Martineau, Samsung SAIL, 2025, arXiv:2510.04871)**: a single 2-layer network, 7M parameters, recursing over a latent z and an answer y, full backprop through the recursion (replacing HRM's one-step implicit-function-theorem gradient raised Sudoku-Extreme from 56.5% to 87.4%). Reported: Sudoku-Extreme 87.4% (HRM 55.0%), Maze-Hard 85.3% (74.5%), ARC-AGI-1 44.6% (40.3%), ARC-AGI-2 7.8% (5.0%). Follow-ups: Probabilistic TRM (Mila, 2026, arXiv:2605.19943), TRM with Mamba-2/attention hybrid (2026, arXiv:2602.12078), Tiny Autoregressive Recursive Models (2026, arXiv:2603.08082). Caveat: TRM inherits HRM's per-puzzle embedding and augmentation regime; ARC-AGI-2 remains near floor.

**Do any of these avoid matmul?** No. Every model in this section is built from dense linear projections (and, for Mamba/RWKV/xLSTM/TTT, matrix-valued recurrent states updated by outer products or delta rules). RWKV-7's per-token state update is a rank-1 modification of a d x d matrix; TTT's state update is a gradient step through a linear/MLP layer; TRM/HRM are small transformers or MLP-mixers. Zero of them is a candidate "no-matmul backbone" as-is. What they *do* offer the team is (a) evidence that fixed-size recurrent state can match attention at <=3B when the state is *matrix-valued and updated by a delta rule* (RWKV-7, Gated DeltaNet, xLSTM), (b) the recall-vs-state-size tradeoff (Based) which says a fixed geometric state *will* lose in-context recall unless paired with an exact external memory, and (c) the HRM post-mortem, which is a template for how the field will audit any small-model "reasoning" claim: replace the exotic component with a plain baseline of equal size, check for per-instance memorization, check held-out semi-private sets.

## C. Discrete, weightless, and logic-gate networks

This is the closest published analogue to "learned lookup tables and finite typed operators."

**Deep Differentiable Logic Gate Networks (Petersen et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2210.08277)**: each neuron is a soft mixture over the 16 two-input Boolean gates with two fixed random inputs; after training it collapses to a hard gate. MNIST ~98% with a network of ~50k gates and > 1M images/s on CPU **[BK]**. **Convolutional DLGNs / LogicTreeNet (Petersen et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2411.04732)**: convolutional logic trees, OR-pooling, residual initialization; CIFAR-10 86.2% (per the structured report), MNIST up to 99.35%, with up to 29x fewer gates than prior efficient models; FPGA implementations at nanosecond-scale latency. Active 2025-26 line: Light DLGNs (ETH, arXiv:2510.03250), LILogic Net (arXiv:2511.12340), ternary-gate surrogates (arXiv:2603.00302), fully trainable connections for LGNs and LUT networks (arXiv:2607.09399), BitLogic training framework (arXiv:2602.07400), FPGN (arXiv:2607.08427), LogicIR for image restoration (arXiv:2606.26609).

**Recurrent DLGNs (Bührer, Plesner, Aczel, Wattenhofer, ETH, 2025, arXiv:2508.06097)** is the *only* sequence-to-sequence language result in this family I could find. WMT'14 En-De, word-level, 16k shared vocab, sequences truncated to 16 tokens: uncollapsed RDDLGN 5.00 BLEU / 30.9% token accuracy; collapsed (hard gates) 4.39 BLEU / 27.7%; baselines RNN 4.59, GRU 5.41, small Transformer 5.98 BLEU. Model: 40.8M parameters of which 16.4M are 1024-dim embeddings; the collapsed logic core is 1.53M "parameters." Notable positive: much better retention on a shifted-copy task (97% at shift <=4 vs. <55% for RNN/GRU). Notable negatives: ~5 BLEU is roughly an order of magnitude below production MT (~28-30 BLEU for full transformers on this task **[BK]**); sequences of 16 tokens; no hardware synthesis yet; a persistent "discretization gap" on collapse.

**Differentiable Weightless Neural Networks (Bacellar et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2410.11112)**: directly trained multi-layer lookup tables (LUT-2 to LUT-6) via an Extended Finite Difference gradient plus learnable input mapping and spectral regularization. FPGA (Zynq Z-7045) results from Table 1: MNIST 98.31% at 19.0 nJ/sample and 125 ns latency (vs. ULEEN 98.46% at 823 nJ, FINN 98.40% at 5445 nJ); FashionMNIST 89.12%; keyword spotting 71.52%; CIFAR-10 only **57.4%** (vs. FINN's convolutional BNN at 80.1%); geometric-mean improvements over ULEEN of 3.3x latency, 2.3x throughput, 19x energy, 22.7x LUT area; an FPGA energy-delay product 2522x better than DiffLogicNet on the native LUT-6 size. Also competitive with XGBoost on tabular data. **ULEEN (Susskind et al., 2023, arXiv:2304.10618)** is the prior weightless SOTA (WiSARD lineage with Bloom-filter RAM nodes); **WiSARD** (Aleksander et al., 1984) is the classic RAM-discriminator model **[BK]**. **TsetlinWiSARD (2026, arXiv:2603.24186)** does on-chip training.

**LUTNet (Wang et al., 2019, arXiv:1904.00938)** and **LogicNets (Umuroglu et al., FPL 2020, arXiv:2004.03021)**: map quantized neurons to K-input truth tables for FPGA soft logic; LogicNets targets extreme-throughput tasks (jet tagging, network intrusion) with tiny inputs. **PolyLUT, NeuraLUT** are later refinements (cited inside DWN's Table 2).

**Tsetlin machines** (Granmo, 2018 **[BK]**) learn conjunctive clauses over Boolean literals with finite-state automata. NLP results exist but are classification-only: sentiment, text classification, word-sense disambiguation, and TM-based word embeddings (Yadav et al., 2021, arXiv:2104.06901; TM Embedding arXiv:2301.00709; Omni TM-AE arXiv:2505.16386; multi-phase word embeddings arXiv:2501.19018; "Clusters are all you need", 2026, arXiv:2606.19815 which imports semantic clusters from a BERT-class model to close the gap). There is no Tsetlin *language model* (next-token generator) with competitive perplexity in the record.

**Scaling limits, stated bluntly.** Across DLGN/DWN/LUTNet/LogicNets/Tsetlin, the best published results in 2024-2026 are: MNIST ~99%, CIFAR-10 57-86% (86% only with convolutional inductive bias and ~millions of gates), keyword spotting ~71%, tabular at XGBoost level, and *one* MT result at 5 BLEU on 16-token sequences. Nobody has published a held-out perplexity on a standard LM corpus (WikiText-103, The Pile, FineWeb) for any of these families. The recurring obstacles are: fixed random connectivity (input selection is the hard part: DWN, LILogic and arXiv:2607.09399 all attack this), vanishing gradients through deep gate stacks (residual initialization in Petersen 2024), the discretization/collapse gap, and routing congestion on real FPGAs for wide layers (DWN Table 1 notes several models could not be placed).

**Lesson.** Learned LUT/logic networks are real and extremely efficient *per inference*, but their demonstrated capability ceiling is roughly "2015-era small CNN on CIFAR" and "2014-era RNN on MT with tiny context." If the team's LUT operators are to do language, they must show a *perplexity* curve versus gate/table count that the community can compare with parameter-count curves, and they should expect to need learnable connectivity, residual/identity initialization, and an explicit treatment of the train-time-soft vs. inference-time-hard gap.

## D. Memory-augmented, retrieval, and exact-copy mechanisms

**kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., ICLR 2020, arXiv:1911.00172)**: interpolating a transformer LM with nearest-neighbour lookup over datastore keys (context representations) reduced WikiText-103 perplexity from 18.65 to 15.79 without extra training **[BK]**, and shows that explicit memorization of rare patterns is cheaper via retrieval than via parameters. **RETRO (Borgeaud et al., DeepMind 2021/22, arXiv:2112.04426)**: chunked cross-attention over a 2T-token retrieval database; a 7.5B RETRO matched GPT-3/Jurassic-1 (175B-class) on The Pile **[BK]**, with the caveat of test-set leakage from the database that the authors themselves audited.

**Large Memory Layers with Product Keys (Lample et al., NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1907.05242)**: a key-value memory with product-quantized keys (search two sqrt(N) sub-key tables instead of N keys) that adds hundreds of millions of parameters at near-zero FLOPs; a 12-layer transformer with one PKM layer outperformed a 24-layer baseline at 2x inference speed **[BK]**. **Memory Layers at Scale (Berges, Oguz et al., Meta FAIR, 2024, arXiv:2412.09764)**: revives PKM with shared memory across ~3 layers, custom kernels at ~3 TB/s (6x over PyTorch EmbeddingBag), input-dependent SiLU gating, qk-norm. Results (from the report): 1.3B base + 1M memory slots matches dense models with 2-4x more compute on QA; factual-QA accuracy more than doubles at fixed base size; 1.3B + 64M keys (128B memory params) approaches Llama2-7B trained on 2x tokens with 10x FLOPs; at 8B + 64B memory params trained to 1T tokens it approaches Llama3.1-8B (15T tokens). Memory+ beat MoE and PEER at matched parameters and compute. **Conditional Memory via Scalable Lookup / Engram (Cheng et al., PKU & DeepSeek, 2026, arXiv:2601.07372)**: modernizes *N-gram hashed embeddings* as an O(1) deterministic-address lookup module; a U-shaped sparsity-allocation law between MoE and lookup memory; scaled to 27B memory parameters, beating an iso-parameter/iso-FLOP MoE baseline (MMLU +3.4, BBH +5.0, ARC-C +3.7, HumanEval +3.0, MATH +2.4, multi-query NIAH 84.2 to 97.0). Deterministic addressing allows prefetch from host memory. **Fast-weight Product Key Memory (Sakana AI, 2026, arXiv:2601.00671)** merges PKM with fast weights. **Memory^3 (2024, arXiv:2407.01178)** externalizes explicit knowledge memories.

**Copy and induction.** Pointer networks / copy mechanisms (Vinyals et al., 2015; Gu et al., 2016; See et al., 2017 **[BK]**) are the classical "Copy" operator. **Induction heads (Olsson et al., Anthropic, 2022, arXiv:2209.11895)**: a two-head circuit implementing [A][B] ... [A] -> [B]; forms in a phase change early in training and coincides with the emergence of in-context learning. Follow-ups: dual-route (token vs. concept) induction (2025, arXiv:2504.03022) and "Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams" (2026, arXiv:2607.02800). Exact copy is a *finite-control* operation given an addressable context: the team's "Read/Copy" operators are exactly what induction heads learn to approximate with soft attention.

**Knowledge capacity.** Allen-Zhu & Li, "Physics of Language Models Part 3.3" (2024, arXiv:2404.05405): controlled synthetic-biography experiments establish that LMs "can and only can store 2 bits of knowledge per parameter, even when quantized to int8"; a 7B model thus stores ~14B bits, exceeding English Wikipedia + textbooks; GPT-2 with RoPE matched or beat LLaMA/Mistral architectures on capacity (GatedMLP is harder to train); prepending domain names raises capacity; in the paper body, int4 quantization cuts capacity by more than 2x and MoE stores knowledge with total, not active, parameters **[BK]**. Corroborating: "Understanding Factual Recall in Transformers via Associative Memories" (2024, arXiv:2412.06538) and "Incompressible Knowledge Probes" (2026, arXiv:2604.24827; log-linear mapping from factual recall to parameter count with R^2 = 0.91 on 93 open models; MoE knowledge tracks total parameters R^2 = 0.67 vs. active 0.41).

**Lesson.** Dense parameters are a ~2-bit/parameter fact store, and the field has now shown, at DeepSeek and Meta scale, that *exact-address lookup memory* (hashed n-grams, product keys) is a better place to store facts than dense weights, while the dense "compute" backbone still does the composing. That is strong support for the team's "exact addressed memory" pillar. The unsolved part is the other half: nobody has yet replaced the dense compute backbone with a finite-state controller and kept quality; Berges et al. explicitly find that replacing *too many* FFN layers with memory degrades results, i.e., dense computation remains load-bearing.

## E. Geometric, hyperbolic, and group-equivariant methods

**Poincaré embeddings (Nickel & Kiela, NeurIPS 2017, arXiv:1705.08039)**: hyperbolic embeddings reconstruct WordNet hierarchies with far fewer dimensions than Euclidean ones **[BK]**. Hyperbolic LMs: **Hyperbolic Fine-Tuning / HypLoRA (Yale et al., 2024, arXiv:2410.04010)**, **HELM: Hyperbolic LLMs via mixture-of-curvature experts (2025, arXiv:2505.24722)**, **HoPE: hyperbolic rotary positional encoding (2025, arXiv:2509.05218)**, **HypRAG (2026, arXiv:2602.07739)** and a survey, "Hyperbolic Deep Learning for Foundation Models" (2025, arXiv:2507.17787). Gains are modest (low single-digit points) and all are implemented with Möbius operations on top of standard matmul layers.

**Geometric Algebra Transformer (Brehmer et al., Qualcomm, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.18415)**: represents inputs as projective geometric algebra multivectors and uses E(3)-equivariant linear maps, geometric products and attention; strong on n-body, arterial wall-shear-stress and robotics tasks **[BK]**. **Clifford Neural Layers for PDE modeling (Brandstetter et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2209.04934)**. **Quaternion RNNs / QCNNs (Parcollet et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv:1806.04418)**: Hamilton-product layers with ~3-4x fewer parameters at equal speech-recognition accuracy **[BK]**. Note that the Hamilton product of two quaternions is a fixed 4x4 bilinear map: it *is* a small matrix product, and quaternion layers are weight-shared real matmuls. **Clifford Algebraic Rotor Embeddings (2025, arXiv:2511.11665)** and **GRAPE (Group Representational Position Encoding, 2025, arXiv:2512.07805)** generalize RoPE to rotor/group actions; "Symmetry in language statistics shapes the geometry of model representations" (2026, arXiv:2602.15029) shows that circular/1-D manifold structure in learned representations arises from data symmetries.

**RoPE (Su et al., 2021, arXiv:2104.09864)**: position n acts on 2-D subspaces of q and k by rotation by n*theta_i, so that <R_m q, R_n k> depends only on m-n. This is the one geometric idea that has become universal in LLMs, and it is *purely a group action on state* (a product of commuting SO(2) rotations), exactly the kind of finite-precision rotation a quaternion/H4 design could implement without a learned matrix. Empirically, RoPE's benefit is relative-position structure and long-range decay, not any particular frequency schedule; "Round and Round We Go" (2024, arXiv:2410.06205) argues the low frequencies carry positional information and high frequencies act as semantic channels **[BK]**.

**Prime- or zeta-based positional encodings.** I searched specifically (Exa, alphaXiv). I found no peer-reviewed or arXiv paper that uses prime numbers or Riemann zeta zeros as positional features or as identity codes in a language model. The closest items are: Algebraic Positional Encodings (NeurIPS 2024; positions as orthogonal group operators), "Alternative positional encoding functions" (2025, arXiv:2512.19323; other periodic bases), a wavelet/Legendre proposal (2025, arXiv:2506.06398), and geometric analyses of PEs (2026, arXiv:2604.05217). There is therefore **no precedent and no evidence** either way for zeta-zero phases; the burden of proof is entirely on the team, and the natural control is "same number of fixed random phases."

**Lesson.** Geometry helps when it encodes a *true symmetry of the data* (3-D rotation for molecules, hierarchy for taxonomies, relative shift for sequences). The published wins are equivariance and RoPE; the published hyperbolic-LM wins are small. A 120-root H4 state or unit quaternions give you a finite, exactly composable rotation group, which is attractive for a no-multiplier implementation; but the literature offers no reason to believe that the *specific* choice of H4 or of prime/zeta features carries linguistic information. Treat them as an engineering substrate (cheap exact group operations) and prove any semantic claim with ablations against random substrates of equal size.

## F. Vector-symbolic / hyperdimensional computing

**Foundations.** Kanerva's Sparse Distributed Memory (1988) and binary spatter codes (XOR binding, majority bundling, permutation for sequence) **[BK]**; Plate's Holographic Reduced Representations (circular convolution binding, IEEE TNN 1995) **[BK]**; Gayler's "VSA answer Jackendoff's challenges" (2003) coined the term VSA **[BK]**. **Kleyko et al., two-part survey (2021/22, arXiv:2111.06077 and arXiv:2112.15424; ACM Computing Surveys)** and "VSA as a Computing Framework for Emerging Hardware" (Kleyko et al., Proc. IEEE 2022, arXiv:2106.05268) are the canonical references.

**Capacity results.** The governing fact is that a D-dimensional binary/bipolar bundle of M items can be queried reliably only while M is roughly O(D / log N) for an N-item codebook, with error growing sharply beyond (Frady, Kleyko & Sommer, "A theory of sequence indexing and working memory in recurrent neural networks", Neural Computation 2018 **[BK]**). "Capacity Analysis of Vector Symbolic Architectures" (Clarkson, Ubaru & Yang, 2023, arXiv:2301.10352) gives formal bounds for four VSAs (including binary MAP-I); "Understanding HDC for parallel single-pass learning" (Yu et al., 2022, arXiv:2202.04805) shows the accuracy ceiling of binary HDC is a limit on what similarity matrices binary vectors can express, and closes the gap with random Fourier features. XOR binding is exactly invertible and distributes over majority only approximately; retrieval from a bundle requires a clean-up memory (nearest-neighbour over the codebook), i.e., an *associative lookup*, which is the expensive step.

**Neuro-VSA reasoning.** Hersche et al., "A Neuro-vector-symbolic Architecture for Solving Raven's Progressive Matrices" (Nature Machine Intelligence 2023, arXiv:2203.04571): a CNN front-end producing VSA object codes plus symbolic probabilistic reasoning in the VSA algebra; 87.7% on RAVEN and 88.1% on I-RAVEN (records at the time), with reasoning two orders of magnitude faster than symbolic search. Follow-ups: LARS-VSA for abstract rules (2024, arXiv:2405.14436), a complete vector-symbolic Lisp (2025, arXiv:2510.17889 and arXiv:2511.08767, with residue arithmetic), "Attention as Binding" (2025, arXiv:2512.14709) reading transformer attention as VSA binding, and "Hyperdimensional Probe" (2025, arXiv:2509.25045) decoding LLM residual streams with VSA. There is no VSA *language model* with competitive perplexity in the literature; the strongest language-adjacent results are n-gram text classification (Kleyko Part II).

**Lesson.** VSA gives the team a mathematically clean vocabulary: XOR = binding, majority = bundling, permutation = sequence, and it is genuinely multiplier-free. Its two hard limits are (i) bundle capacity scales linearly in D and only logarithmically in codebook size, so a 10k-dim binary state holds on the order of a few hundred bound items, not a document; and (ii) every retrieval needs a clean-up (nearest-neighbour) step that is a matrix-vector product unless the codebook is addressable (hashing, Hamming-code syndrome decoding). The second point is precisely where the team's Hamming-code / exact-address design could be a real contribution: *exactly decodable* codes replace approximate clean-up.

## G. Expressivity theory

**Transformers are (roughly) TC0.** Merrill & Sabharwal, "The Parallelism Tradeoff: Limitations of Log-Precision Transformers" (TACL 2023, arXiv:2207.00729): log-precision transformers are simulable by constant-depth uniform threshold circuits, so under L != P they cannot solve linear equalities or general CFG membership; the "parallelism tradeoff" says any architecture as parallelizable as the transformer inherits the same limits. "A Logic for Expressing Log-Precision Transformers" (Merrill & Sabharwal, NeurIPS 2023) tightens this to first-order logic with majority quantifiers. **Liu et al., "Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata" (ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.10749)**: o(T)-depth transformers can simulate any finite automaton on length-T inputs via Krohn-Rhodes decompositions; O(log T) depth always suffices and O(1) depth is common, but the learned shortcuts are brittle out of distribution. **Strobl, Merrill, Weiss, Chiang & Angluin, "What Formal Languages Can Transformers Express? A Survey" (TACL 2024, arXiv:2311.00208)** harmonizes the assumptions (hard vs. soft attention, precision, positional encodings, intermediate steps). **RASP / "Thinking Like Transformers" (Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav, ICML 2021, arXiv:2106.06981)** gives the programming-language view. **Merrill & Sabharwal, "The Expressive Power of Transformers with Chain of Thought" (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.07923)**: log-many intermediate steps add little; a *linear* number of steps lets decoders recognize all regular languages (and stays within context-sensitive); polynomially many steps with generalized pre-norm recognize exactly P.

**Recurrent alternatives.** RWKV-7 (above) and Gated DeltaNet-style models claim to recognize all regular languages with parallelizable training, i.e., they escape TC0 into NC1-type state tracking, which transformers without CoT cannot do under standard conjectures.

**Finite control + exact external memory.** Classical results **[BK, standard theory]**: a finite-state controller with two stacks (two-stack PDA), or with a read/write tape, is Turing complete; a DFA alone recognizes exactly the regular languages; a single-stack PDA the context-free ones. Siegelmann & Sontag (1992/1995) showed that recurrent nets with rational weights and unbounded precision are Turing complete and with real weights super-Turing; the precision requirement is exactly what finite-state hardware lacks, which is why *unbounded exact memory* (not precision) is the honest route to expressivity. Learned neural stacks/tapes (Graves 2014 NTM; Joulin & Mikolov 2015 stack RNNs; DeLeTang et al., 2022, "Neural networks and the Chomsky hierarchy" **[BK]**) show that memory-augmented RNNs generalize on formal tasks far better than transformers and LSTMs, but that *learning the controller* by gradient descent is the fragile part.

**Implication for "finite geometric state + exact addressed memory + learned finite control".** On paper this is the strongest position in the expressivity landscape: a finite controller with random-access exact memory is Turing complete, and unlike CoT transformers it does not need to serialize computation into O(n) emitted tokens. But two things follow. First, expressivity is *not* the bottleneck for prose quality: TC0-bounded transformers write far better prose than every Turing-complete neural machine ever built; the bottleneck is *learnability at scale from data*. Second, the results of Liu et al. and the HRM audit show that when you give a model a shortcut (depth, per-instance embeddings), it takes the shortcut; a finite controller learned by gradient descent will memorize rather than generalize unless the training distribution forces algorithmic solutions (length extrapolation tests are mandatory).

## H. The small local frontier (2025-2026)

**Models.** Qwen3-4B (Qwen3 Technical Report, 2025, arXiv:2505.09388; pretraining on ~36T tokens across 119 languages with thinking/non-thinking modes; the report positions Qwen3-4B as roughly matching Qwen2.5-7B **[BK]**). Phi-4-mini 3.8B (Microsoft, 2025, arXiv:2503.01743; reported MMLU 67.3, HumanEval 74.4, GSM8K 88.6 [web: third-party compilations of the model card]). Gemma 3 4B (Google, 2025; MMLU 59.6; IT variant GSM8K 89.2, HumanEval 71.3 [web]) and Gemma 3n E2B/E4B (2025; per-layer embeddings, ~4B effective of 8B total; first sub-10B model above 1300 LMArena Elo [web]). SmolLM3-3B (Hugging Face, 2025; 11.2T tokens, fully open recipe; beats Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B across 12 benchmarks [web]). Llama 3.2 3B (Meta, 2024; MMLU 63.4 [web]). By September 2026 the community is already moving to Qwen3.5-4B (reported MMLU-Pro 79.1 [web]) and Gemma 4 E4B; a controlled 8,400-evaluation study (arXiv:2604.07035, 2026) finds Gemma-4-E4B with few-shot CoT the best accuracy/VRAM operating point among seven dense and MoE small reasoning models, and finds prompt-format sensitivity so large that Phi-4-reasoning drops from 0.67 to 0.11 on GSM8K between two prompting protocols.

**What "quality" means at <=4B.** These models are trained on 4-36 trillion tokens with ~10^23-10^24 FLOPs. They are strong on GSM8K (~78-90%), moderate on MMLU (~60-70%), and far from frontier on GPQA-Diamond, AIME, SWE-bench-class coding, and long-form factuality. "Frontier-quality" at <=4B is not achieved by *anyone*, with or without matmul.

**Throughput and energy on Apple Silicon.** Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: each token streams the entire weight set once. Base M1 has 68.25 GB/s spec (~61 GB/s achievable). Measured [web: ziraph.com, June 2026]: a 12B model at ~4.9 bits/weight decodes at 7-7.8 tok/s on M1 with both llama.cpp and MLX, both pinned at 93-97% of the bandwidth ceiling; measured energy ~1.0-1.4 J/token whole-system. A 4B model at Q4 (~2.3-2.7 GB) therefore has a *bandwidth ceiling* of roughly 22-27 tok/s on base M1 (my computation from those numbers), consistent with community reports of ~20-30 tok/s for 3-4B Q4 models on M1. On M4 Max [web: github.com/n0an/apple-silicon-llm-bench, 2026]: Qwen3-4B ~145 tok/s (MLX), Gemma 4 E4B 80-114 tok/s, Gemma 4 E2B 0.24-0.25 J/token at ~24.5 W package power for GPU runtimes and 0.11 J/token for Apple's on-device foundation model; the ANE path draws half the power (12.7 W) but is *worse* per token (0.48 J) because it is slower. [web: AtomGradient study of Qwen3.5-4B on Apple Silicon] Q8_0 costs 0.18% perplexity, Q6_K 0.54%, Q4_K_M 4.07%, Q2_K +267% perplexity, i.e., sub-4-bit PTQ is catastrophic without QAT. BitNet b1.58 2B4T via bitnet.cpp on the *same* M1 is the correct no-multiplier baseline; T-MAC reports 30 tok/s single-core for a 3B ternary model on M2 Ultra.

**Lesson.** On M1, a 4B-class model already runs at ~25 tok/s and ~1 J/token; the *practical* win available from eliminating multipliers is bounded by bandwidth, not arithmetic: a ternary 4B model is ~0.8 GB and could stream at ~75 tok/s on base M1 if the kernel is bandwidth-bound. Any design whose "weights" (LUTs, codebooks, memories) exceed a few GB will be *slower* than the matmul incumbents on this hardware regardless of arithmetic cost. Report tok/s, J/token (powermetrics), and resident bytes/token together.

## I. Methodology norms for a credible LM result

1. **Held-out perplexity in bits-per-byte (BPB), not token perplexity**, so that tokenizer/vocabulary choices do not confound (Paloma, Magnusson et al., 2023, arXiv:2312.10523, evaluates fit across 546 domains with controlled 1B baselines and shows single-corpus perplexity does not transfer across domains). Report on a public corpus (FineWeb-Edu, The Pile subsets, WikiText-103) with decontamination against the training set.
2. **Compute- and data-matched baselines.** Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 2022, arXiv:2203.15556) set the norm: compare at equal training FLOPs and tokens, and fit scaling curves rather than reporting one point. The xLSTM scaling-law paper and the MatMul-free LM paper both use IsoFLOP/parametric fits; the HRM audit used a *same-size transformer with the same outer loop* as the control.
3. **Downstream suite**, standardized harness (lm-eval-harness or equivalent), reported with the exact prompt format, few-shot count, and normalization, because prompt protocol alone can move GSM8K by 50 points (arXiv:2604.07035).
4. **Ablations that remove the novel component and replace it with a random or trivial one of equal size** (random phases instead of zeta phases; random rotations instead of H4; hashed random tables instead of learned tables). The HRM case is the cautionary tale: the headline component contributed almost nothing.
5. **Leakage audits.** Sainz et al., "NLP Evaluation in Trouble" (2023, arXiv:2310.18018) argues contamination must be measured per benchmark; Zhang et al., GSM1k (2024, arXiv:2405.00332) built a fresh 1,000-problem clone of GSM8K and found accuracy drops of up to 8% (some families 13%) with systematic overfitting; the RETRO paper audited retrieval-database overlap. For a memory-heavy architecture the risk is worse: an exact-address memory *is* a copy of training data, so any evaluation on text with n-gram overlap to the memory contents is contaminated by construction. Report overlap statistics (13-gram or longer) between eval sets and memory contents.
6. **Length and distribution extrapolation** for any "reasoning" or "algorithmic" claim (train on <=n, test on 2n), following the formal-languages literature.
7. **Pitfalls of template-generated synthetic evals.** Templates have low entropy: a model can reach ~100% by learning the template rather than the task; per-instance identifiers (the HRM `puzzle_id`) turn "reasoning" into table lookup; answer-format artifacts inflate exact-match; and models trained on synthetic data from the same generator see the test distribution during training. Use templates only for *diagnostics with reported template count and slot entropy*, never as headline numbers; pair every synthetic result with a natural-data result.
8. **Measured, not estimated, efficiency.** Wall-clock tok/s, powermetrics J/token, resident memory, on named hardware and OS version, against the incumbent (llama.cpp Q4 and bitnet.cpp ternary) on the same machine.

## Lessons for a no-matmul geometric LM team

1. **Decide what "no matmul" means and write it down.** If it bans the *mathematical* linear map, then BitNet, MatMul-free LM, T-MAC and ShiftAddLLM are not precedents and you are in genuinely unexplored territory; if it bans multiplier *hardware*, they are your direct competitors and BitNet b1.58 2B4T on bitnet.cpp is the baseline you must beat on the same M1.
2. **The bandwidth wall, not arithmetic, sets your speed on M1.** ~61 GB/s means ~25 tok/s for a 2.5 GB model regardless of how cheap each operation is. Every LUT, codebook and memory byte you touch per token counts against that budget. Design for bytes-per-token first.
3. **Nobody has produced a competitive LM from learned logic gates or LUTs.** The best published sequence result is 5 BLEU on 16-token WMT'14 (RDDLGN, 2025); the best vision result is CIFAR-10 at 57-86%. You are attempting something with a demonstrated gap of one to two orders of magnitude; plan milestones (WikiText-103 BPB at 10M, 100M "gates") that let you see the scaling slope early.
4. **Exact addressed memory is the best-supported pillar in your design.** Memory Layers at Scale and DeepSeek's Engram show that O(1) deterministic-address lookup memory beats dense and MoE parameters for knowledge at matched cost, and Allen-Zhu & Li quantify why (dense weights store ~2 bits/parameter). Build the memory first, measure its bits/byte and hit rate, then worry about the controller.
5. **But the dense backbone is still load-bearing.** Berges et al. find quality falls when too many FFN layers become memory; Based finds fixed-state models fail at in-context recall. The open problem is replacing *composition* (not storage) with finite control. Expect this to be where you spend most of your effort and where the risk is.
6. **Fixed-size recurrent state works only when updated by a delta rule on a matrix-valued state.** RWKV-7, Gated DeltaNet, xLSTM and TTT all reach transformer parity at <=3B with state updates that are rank-1 outer products. A quaternion or H4 state is tiny by comparison; you will need many of them, or an external memory to compensate, and you must test in-context recall (needle, MQAR) explicitly.
7. **Expressivity is not your bottleneck; learnability is.** A finite controller with exact random-access memory is Turing complete, but TC0-bounded transformers write better prose than every Turing-complete neural machine ever trained. Prove you can *learn* the controller from data at scale, with length extrapolation, before claiming any advantage from expressivity.
8. **Group-structured state is a substrate, not a linguistic theory.** RoPE and equivariant networks win because they encode a real symmetry of the data. There is no literature support for H4/120-root, prime identities or zeta-zero phases carrying linguistic information; run the "same-size random substrate" ablation before believing any of them matter.
9. **VSA capacity is linear in dimension and logarithmic in codebook size.** A 10k-bit bundle holds hundreds of items, not a document, and every retrieval needs clean-up. Your Hamming-code exact decoding is the one place you can beat standard VSA (exact vs. approximate clean-up); quantify it.
10. **Learn from the HRM audit, because it will be applied to you.** Replace your novel component with a plain baseline of equal size; check for per-instance memorization; evaluate on a hidden set; expect the community to find that the outer training loop, augmentation, or memory did the work. Do this audit yourselves first.
11. **Ternary accumulate on CPUs is the incumbent no-multiplier kernel.** T-MAC's bit-serial LUT trick (30 tok/s single core for 3B ternary on M2 Ultra) is the efficiency bar for any lookup-based operator. If your Read/Copy/Add/Emit operators cannot match its bytes-per-token and adds-per-token, geometry will not save you.
12. **Report BPB, not perplexity; measured joules, not operator counts; matched-compute baselines, not "trained on less."** Both BitNet reports estimate energy; the M1 community measures it. Be the ones who measure.
13. **Treat your memory as contamination by construction.** An exact-address memory filled from training text makes any eval text with n-gram overlap contaminated. Publish overlap statistics and use fresh, post-cutoff eval data (GSM1k-style clones).
14. **Do not lead with template-generated synthetic evals.** They are diagnostics with low slot entropy; the field now discounts them heavily after HRM and after GSM8K contamination studies. Every synthetic headline must be paired with a natural-data number.
15. **"Frontier quality at 4B" does not exist yet for anyone.** Qwen3-4B, Gemma 3n E4B, Phi-4-mini and SmolLM3 cost 4-36T tokens and still sit at ~60-70 MMLU and near-zero on frontier reasoning suites. Set the near-term target at "SmolLM3/Qwen3-4B-class BPB and GSM8K on M1 at >=25 tok/s and <1 J/token, without a multiplier," which would itself be a first.
