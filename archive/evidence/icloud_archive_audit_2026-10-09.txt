UOR-R4 — iCloud archive audit: three blind spots, a defective backing store, and the disk arithmetic
Date: 2026-10-09. DeepSeek session (owner instruction: "make sure all of cloudstore is pushed to icloud").
References #820.

WHAT WAS DONE
  Verified and extended the project's iCloud archive (cloud-store). Index went from 397 to 572 objects:
    61  this session's artifacts - every panel, every exported multiplier-free LUT, all 25 checkpoints
        (fetch-verified: an object was pulled back down, MD5-checked and extracted to the right path)
    ~10  working corpora and gap-list directories: chat-v0-p2, inputs/*, step0/*, step4,
        step2-parity-bench, query-typing, state-probe
    3   stale archives refreshed under dated names
    12  workspace sub-directories
    41  uor-r4-lab sub-directories
  Nothing was deleted or overwritten. The only removals were staging copies already verified and
  indexed, plus 1.8 GB of local staging, which took free disk from 12 to 18 GiB.

THE TWO FINDINGS THAT MATTER
  1. THE CHAT CORPUS HAD NO ARCHIVE. chat-v0-p2 - the 253 MB corpus every experiment in this session
     ran on - had NO entry anywhere in the index, while the store looked complete by object count.
     Same for the whole of inputs/ and step0/ (4.4 GB between them). The store was not as complete as
     its 397 objects suggested.
  2. THE LARGEST ARCHIVE WAS ON THE FAILING DEVICE. workspace's 42 existing objects resolve to
     icloud:X10 - the X10 Pro SSD whose defective flash region is the reason iCloud became the
     project's authoritative storage in the first place. So the project's largest local directory was
     archived onto the suspect device. Its contents now exist on iCloud proper as 12
     ws-*-20261009 objects, and because cloud-store never overwrites, the old archive is preserved.

THREE AUDIT BLIND SPOTS, EACH INVISIBLE TO THE PREVIOUS CHECK
  * NAME MATCHING misses CONTAINERS: inputs/ and step0/ are directories of other labs' directories, so
    "is inputs archived?" is the wrong question - their CONTENTS had to be checked individually. The
    name-based audit reported 19 "gaps" of which most were containers already covered.
  * TIMESTAMPS catch STALE ARCHIVES: a directory archived on 2 October while being written until the
    4th looks present and complete. cloud-store REFUSES TO OVERWRITE ("already in the store; never
    overwritten"), so catching up requires a DATED NAME - which is why the store already contained
    objects like workspace-2026-10-01. Three directories were two days stale this way.
  * LAB LABELS hide directories archived as many labelled objects rather than one named object:
    workspace appears under lab workspace-2026-10-01 with 42 objects and no object named "workspace".
  Both error directions occur: name matching made the store look LESS complete than it is (containers),
  and timestamp blindness made it look MORE complete (staleness).

THE DISK ARITHMETIC - AND AN EMERGENCY CAUGHT
  cloud-store stages a tar EQUAL TO THE DIRECTORY SIZE, so a push needs about TWICE its size free.
  Pushing workspace (6.7 GB) in one operation drove free disk from 18 GiB to 5.0 GiB; the job was killed
  and staging removed, restoring 18 GiB. Failing to catch it would have filled the disk and endangered
  other labs' work. The project rule that makes this the RIGHT cancellation: the disk floor is an owner
  cap, explicitly outside the "never cancel a healthy run" protection.
  THE FIX IS STRUCTURAL, not care: recurse to sub-directory granularity and check
      push only when 2 * size + 10 GiB <= free
  before each object. With that guard, workspace pushed as 12 objects (1 principled skip, logged with
  numbers: uor-r4-lab needed 20,854 MB against 18,673 MB free) and uor-r4-lab then pushed as 41 objects
  with ZERO skips and ZERO failures - including a 2.3 GB piece. Every skip is logged with its numbers.

RESIDUE, RECORDED HONESTLY
  One 16 KB staging shell (workspace-20261009) remains and is NOT removable by this session: it
  captured a snapshot-preserved directory whose files are owned with restrictive permissions. It holds
  no unique data (workspace's real contents are the 12 ws-* objects). It was left alone deliberately -
  forcing deletion of a path owned by another lab's preserved material is exactly the unilateral move
  the preservation rules exist to prevent. It needs the owner of those files, or an escalated removal.

SCOPE
  Archive verification and extension, no model work. Object counts and timestamps are the tool's own
  index; the fetch round trip was tested independently. No file outside the archive was modified and no
  archived object was deleted or overwritten.
