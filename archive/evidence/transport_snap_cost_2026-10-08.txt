UOR-R4 — What it costs to train in the served transport representation
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 24-27). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE COMPARISON
  Same 19.9 M chat stack, same corpus, same steps (12,207), same batch/lr/warmup, same pod, same
  binary. The only difference is one flag: `transport_snap=icosian`.

    arm                       GPU utilisation   first eval (step 4,000)   checkpoints
    continuous transport      100 %             reached in ~150 s          yes
    transport_snap=icosian    15-25 %           not reached in ~25 min     none

  Process evidence for the snapped arm: 336-337 % CPU, 38-40 minutes of CPU time inside 11-12
  minutes of wall time, GPU holding 4,498 MiB but idle 75-85 % of the time. The projection is not
  a rounding error: the snap moves work onto the CPU critical path every step, so the same run
  that finishes in about eleven minutes in the continuous representation has not reached a third
  of its steps in twenty-five.

WHAT THAT MEANS AS A TRADE
  Round 24 established `transport_snap=icosian` as the candidate fix for the largest remaining
  served term, 0.03578 BPB - 64.8 % of the whole served gap - on the reasoning that a model
  trained in the representation it is served in should not pay to be snapped into it.
  This round prices that fix: roughly an order of magnitude of training throughput.

  For a project whose stated objective includes lower energy and wasted compute, that is not a
  free flag. The honest trade is 0.03578 BPB of served fidelity against an order-of-magnitude
  training cost, and the two cannot be weighed without the fidelity number, which the run has not
  produced yet.

THE PRACTICAL FORM OF THE IDEA, IF THE FIDELITY GAIN IS REAL
  The cost is in *training* with the snap, not in *serving* it. If the fidelity gain holds, the
  cheap way to buy it may be a short snapped fine-tune from a model trained in the continuous
  representation - enough steps to move the weights into the snapped basin, rather than 12,207
  steps paying the slow path throughout. That is a testable variant: take the round-16 checkpoint,
  fine-tune with `transport_snap=icosian` for a small step count, then `snap-evaluate` and
  `d11-evaluate`. If `snapped_nll` collapses toward `fused_nll` in a few hundred steps, the cost
  of the flag is paid once and briefly instead of throughout training.

  This document does not claim that happens. It records the measurement, the trade it prices, and
  the cheapest next test.

SCOPE
  One model, one corpus, one pod, single seed, two runs differing only in `transport_snap`. The
  throughput figures are process and GPU observations, not a benchmark harness; the "not reached"
  entries are absences of output, recorded as absences rather than estimated.
