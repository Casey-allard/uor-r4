UOR-R4 — Size at ten times: the 214 M chat stack loses to a 7.16 M chat-only model
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight round 35). DeepSeek session. References #820.

WHAT WAS MEASURED
  The private store holds chat-trained 214 M geometric stacks. `models/chat-214m-smoltalk-b16`
  (pattern rrarrarrarrarrar, width 1536, heads 24, context 384, read L2, pointer model) was scored on
  chat-v0-p2/heldout - sha256 e5f400b0..., the same stream every number in this session uses -
  under the repository's own sealed `evaluate` protocol: consecutive blocks of 256 inputs and 256
  shifted targets, fresh state per block.

    full  nll 2.1568440326982325   over 6,194,304 targets (16,131 blocks)   = 1.09655 bits/byte
    tune  nll 1.9492776837467096   over    24,576 targets                   = 0.99103 bits/byte

  The full-stream figure is the comparable one: the sealed league table was produced with the same
  protocol and the same target count.

THE COMPARISON

  model                        parameters    training data            bits/byte (chat heldout)
  served artifact today             2.7 MB   TinyStories                        4.3885
  Kneser-Ney 5-gram                     --   chat-v0-p2 train                   1.2803
  sealed geo-8m-l2               7,155,396   TinyStories/TinyDialogues/chat     1.1199
  chat-214m-smoltalk              ~214 M     SmolTalk-derived chat mix          1.0966
  control, round 12              7,155,396   chat-v0-p2 train only              0.9607
  round 16 arm                  19,929,136   chat-v0-p2 train only              0.8770

FINDING
  Ten times the parameters with a different chat mixture loses by 0.1359 BPB to a model one
  thirtieth its size trained on this corpus, and by 0.2196 BPB to a 19.9 M model on the same data.
  Round 12 measured the data term at twice the size term (0.1592 against 0.0837 BPB) over a 7.16 M
  to 19.93 M range; this extends the comparison an order of magnitude further and finds the ordering
  unchanged and the margin larger, not smaller. Within this project's corpora and at this scale,
  matching the training distribution is worth more than adding parameters by a wide margin.

  It also means the project's largest chat model - 214 M parameters, trained on a SmolTalk mixture -
  is not the best chat model it has. That is a fact about training data, not about the architecture;
  the same family at 19.9 M on chat-v0-p2 beats it.

SCOPE AND CAVEATS, STATED BECAUSE THE PROTOCOLS DIFFER
  * The 214 M figure is a full-stream sealed-protocol evaluation over 6,194,304 targets. The 19.9 M
    and 7.16 M rows come from snap-evaluate over 512 windows (196,608 targets) and are the models'
    fused float scores, not served scores. The rows are therefore not on one identical protocol and
    the exact deltas should be read as approximate; the ORDERING is the finding, and it is large
    relative to any protocol difference this session has measured.
  * The 214 M model is a pointer model, which is why it was scored with `evaluate` rather than
    `snap-evaluate`; its mixture head means the plain snapped score is not defined for it.
  * Training mixtures for the store models are taken from their manifests, not re-derived here.

CONSEQUENCE
  The data lever is the one worth unblocking, and it is currently blocked: no chat corpus larger
  than the 82 M-token chat-v0-p2 train is reachable in the store or on the canonical volume. This
  measurement is the strongest argument yet for spending the next effort there rather than on a
  larger rung of the same mixture.
