UOR-R4 — Span weighting does not close the continuation gap: three objective variants tried, the mechanism is what remains
Date: 2026-10-09 (overnight rounds 165-167). DeepSeek session. References #820.

WHAT RAN
  The objective half of the probe's recommendation: UOR_COMMITMENT_SPAN=3 (the response's first three
  supervised positions take the commitment weight, the change merged as #1984) with
  UOR_COMMITMENT_WEIGHT=100 and UOR_TERMINAL_WEIGHT=30, on the content-copy store mixture
  (1,006 documents, turn-1 answers the premise's own token span, no acknowledgements), 19,929,136
  parameters, policy=full_prefix, 12,207 steps, panel v2. Development response nll 4.7599 at step 2,000,
  far above the earlier arms' 2.0-2.5, which is the expected signature of concentrating 100x weight on
  three positions and is a dose indicator rather than a metric.

  SO COMPLIANCE IS PARTIAL AND THE RETENTION ANSWER IS STILL NOT THE GIVEN VALUE. The name turn

WHAT THREE OBJECTIVE VARIANTS TOGETHER ESTABLISH
  1. WEIGHTING THE ANSWER REGION DOES NOT CLOSE THE CONTINUATION GAP. Three variants have now been run
     on this mixture: terminal weighting (#1946, dose curve 20/60/100/200 on the earlier store),
     commitment weighting on the first token (#1977, shown by the probe to be a REAL first-token lever
     - the onset becomes value-conditional at p=0.894 with DELTA up to +10.8 nats), and now the same
     weight extended to the first three positions (#1984). The probe's channels put the span at or
     above TAU in 62 of 63 cells and the emitted value in 0 of 63; this arm does not change that
     picture, and its name-bearing reply is again onset-shaped rather than value-shaped.
  2. THE FAILURE IS THEREFORE IN WHAT THE MODEL DOES WITH A SPAN IT ALREADY ADDRESSES. Retrieval is
     present, the onset responds to the value, and the continuation follows the training mixture's
     taught surfaces ("India.", "Hi <name>", number words, store family fragments) rather than the
     span. Expressing that as a weighting problem has now been tried three ways; the evidence says the
     next candidate must change the MECHANISM rather than the loss weights.
  3. THE CANDIDATES, IN THE ORDER THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS THEM: (i) an explicit copy/pointer pathway -
     ALL ARMS TO DATE ARE POINTERLESS, the serving stack already has a pointer mode, and the review
     ranked a pointer below the weighting proposals precisely because it supplies the capacity to copy
     rather than the incentive, which is now the thing that has been shown to be missing; (ii) a
     constrained or two-token decoder that forces the answer to be drawn from the span; (iii) the
     project's unused geometric span/address lane (geometric_address.rs, geometric_span.rs are
     registered but consumed only by standalone examples, and the review's missing pieces are span
     construction, question-to-span addressing, and a token-identity readout).
  4. WHAT NOT TO DO, REINFORCED: no further weight tuning on this axis (three variants tried), no more
     capacity (three sizes tried), no more dependency-carrying stores of the same design (four store
     designs tried), and no selection on development nll (which here moved by 2x while the panel moved
     by much less).

HONEST POSITION
  This arm is a NEGATIVE for the span-weighting hypothesis and is recorded as such. It is the fourth
  route exhausted for binding and the third objective variant ruled out. Meanwhile the night's solved
  result is unaffected: instruction-form compliance remains ten of ten with correct content on the
  demand-store configurations, and the copy-store arms continue to show partial compliance (yes/no
  correct, numeric forms, a number word, a name-bearing greeting) rather than the prose of the
  pre-breakthrough arms.

SCOPE
  One span, one weight pair, one store, one shape, single seed, greedy decoding, one panel read as the
  first two content tokens. Exact for this panel. The probe channels have NOT yet been run on this
  checkpoint and are the obvious next measurement if the pointer route is to be compared fairly.
