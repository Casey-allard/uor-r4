THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
  In all five class-C cells the pointer does NOT lock onto the value's first token: it emits " My name" reading a different occurrence of " name" in the question, then RE-ACQUIRES the premise at window[6] - the copula " is" - and sweeps one window position per step to window[11]. The span the pointer actually locked onto is therefore window[6..11] = " is Ithmar.", one id BEFORE the value span [7..10], and it does not normalise to the value. Measured: the headline rule (mid-reply pointer-locked runs, anchor kept) fires on exactly those 5 cells at every floor and gains ZERO - 72/84 at floor 0.5, 72/84 at 0.75, 71/84 at 1.0, identical to the baselines, 0 cells gained, 0 lost. The only way to reach the value is to DROP the id at which the pointer re-acquired the region; that convention is fitted to these 5 cells after seeing their sealed traces, and it must be labelled fitted wherever its number appears. With it the fitted variant measures 77/84 = 0.9167 (+5 gained / 0 lost, exact McNemar p=0.0625, Newcombe 95 % [-0.0391, +0.1594]) - a better point estimate, NOT an improvement - and at floors 0.75 and 1.0 the fitted variant is +4 / 0 with one template firing per floor. So: the natural reading of "emit only the span the pointer selected" is closed as a negative, and the +5 ceiling quoted by the previous round is not reachable by it.

THE RULE (read-out only, no training, no parameters, nothing serialized, default off)
  Definitions, from the channels the driver already records: a step is LOCKED when the emitted id is
  the window id at the pointer's own selected source (`matches_source`); a POINTER-LOCKED RUN is a
  maximal sequence of consecutive locked steps whose source advances by exactly one each step; a run
  that opens at the reply's first step OPENS AT THE REPLY START, otherwise MID-REPLY; the run's
  SPAN is window[first..last], its POST-ANCHOR SPAN is window[first+1..last].
  `pointer_span_extract=0|1|2|3` (default 0) with `pointer_span_min=N` (default 2, the minimum
  number of EMITTED ids after the anchor convention):
    1 = mid-reply runs, ANCHOR KEPT   -> the HEADLINE (the natural reading; the rule emits exactly
        what the pointer locked onto);
    2 = mid-reply runs, ANCHOR DROPPED -> the FITTED VARIANT;
    3 = any run, anchor kept          -> the BROAD CONTROL (isolates the mid-reply gate).
  When a run qualifies, the reply's ids become exactly its span (everything before the run and after
  it is dropped), `reply_eos=false`, the stop record becomes `{"pointer_span": {...}}` and a
  `span_extract` record field is written. When none qualifies the reply is unchanged, field for
  field; with the rule off no field is added at all (the sealed record schema exactly). The rule
  sees only the emitted ids, the window ids and the pointer's own per-step selection - never the
  row's expected/forbid, the recorded span positions, the record id or any label. Edge cases: a step
  with no selection is not locked; the EOS id never joins a run; a span shorter than `pointer_span_min`
  does not fire. Library API: `StackModel::set_pointer_span_extract(Option<SpanExtract>)`,
  `SpanExtract::{mid_reply_kept, mid_reply_dropped, any_run_kept, from_flag, find}`,
  `Reply::span_extract`.

WHY THE MID-REPLY GATE IS SAFE HERE (measured before the runs, from the sealed traces)
  A structural scan of the sealed idstop3 traces found EXACTLY 5 of 84 cells with a mid-reply
  pointer-locked run of at least 2 ids, and they are exactly the 5 class-C cells; their runs are
  window[6..11] (four cells) and window[6..12] (orvynn). No currently-exact cell has a mid-reply run
  of length >= 2 (four exact cells have length-1 mid-reply runs). The runs confirm it independently:
  mode 1 and mode 2 fire on exactly those 5 cells at all three floors. A mid-reply-only rule
  therefore cannot touch the other 79 cells by construction.

WHAT THIS CLOSES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
  1. CLOSED (negative): span extraction in its natural reading does not recover the class-C cells.
     The pointer's own lock starts at the copula " is", one id before the value, in all five cells.
  2. The fitted variant's +5/84 and +4/84 are better point estimates only; none is significant, and
     the template firing at floors 0.75/1.0 is a reported failure of the same criterion the headline
     failed there.
  3. The 4 class-A cells (value never emitted) remain untouched by any serving-time read-out, as the
     classification said.
  4. NOT measured: other panels, non-greedy decoding, other arms for the rule, any integer/D11 port
     (export still refuses a pointer with an identity term).
  5. INFERRED, not measured: that a read-out using an independent marker of where the value begins
     (none was found in the pointer's own selection on this panel) is what would be needed; the
     anchor's position carries no other signal - attention, floored gate and raw gate at the
     re-acquisition step do not separate it from the value's first token in all five cells.
