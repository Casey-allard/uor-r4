UOR-R4 — Cross-turn attribute binding is not bought by 1.7x capacity: it is a mechanism question, not a scale one
Date: 2026-10-08 (overnight rounds 143-146). DeepSeek session. References #820.

THE TEST
  Exactly the mixture of round 143 - the 1,100-document short-premise attribute store with 9,360
  response tokens, inside the compliant envelope - at 33,773,500 parameters (layers 10, width 640,
  heads 10) against 19,929,136 for the arm that isolated the gap. Same hash-proven chat corpus, same
  mask, policy=full_prefix, UOR_TERMINAL_WEIGHT=60, greedy decoding, panel v2 read as the first two
  content tokens.

RESULT

  form compliance - holds
    "water." "Yes." "No." "3." "Fifteen." "Neutral." "Nile." "Nile."
    seven of fourteen items in the demanded short form, including yes/no with two correct facts

  content - still ungrounded
    "water." for the colour of grass, "3." for the days in a week, "Fifteen." for two plus two

  retention - STILL NOT ANSWERED, AND STILL IN THE STORE'S OWN REGISTER
    "Noted." and "I see." - both turn-1 acknowledgements from this store's own family, not answers
    and not corpus behaviour. At 19.9 M the same two turns returned "Noted." and "Noted."; 1.7x the
    parameters changed the acknowledgement variant and not the failure.

WHAT THIS SETTLES
  1. CROSS-TURN ATTRIBUTE BINDING IS NOT A CAPACITY PROBLEM IN THIS RANGE. A 70 % parameter increase
     leaves it exactly where it was - the model reproduces the mixture's surface register where an
     attribute was requested. That closes the fork the previous round left open, and rules out the
     last "more of the same" remedy on this axis.
  2. FORM COMPLIANCE AND BINDING HAVE DIFFERENT CAUSES, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS DATA. Form compliance
     was bought by DATA SHAPE - a demand and its matching short answer in the same turn - and is
     robust across four configurations, two shapes, two terminal weights, and a 70 % smaller store.
     Binding has now survived three data designs AND a capacity increase, so the remaining candidates
     are the OBJECTIVE and the ARCHITECTURE, which is where the session's central finding already
     points: the per-token next-token objective over a mixed-length corpus values continuation, and
     the model has never been given a reason to bind a question to the span its answer depends on.
  3. THE OBSERVABLE IS SHARP ENOUGH TO TEST A MECHANISM CHANGE. The failure is not diffuse: the model
     answers with the store's own acknowledgement tokens ("Noted.", "I see.") where a recalled
     attribute was asked for, and with the count family's canonical answer ("3.") for every numeric
     item. A mechanism change that makes the model condition on the relevant span should move those
     specific behaviours, and the instrument that reads them is frozen.

WHAT THE PROJECT CARRIES FORWARD
  * SOLVED AND ROBUST: instruction-form compliance. Ten of ten form-demanded turns with correct
    content for the factual classes, from a store of about a thousand documents, at 19.9 M, with
    UOR_TERMINAL_WEIGHT=60. Served under the native contract at 0.93277 bits/byte, a 4.70x reduction,
    with a multiplier-free engine exact to 1e-6 nats.
  * NAMED AND ISOLATED: cross-turn attribute binding, with FOUR failed routes documented - dependency
    carrying documents, longer premises, shorter premises at 70 % less mass, and now 1.7x capacity.
  * The remaining work is therefore a mechanism or objective change for binding, not more data, more
    scale or a better store. That is a materially different and better-posed problem than the one this
    session opened with, and it is the honest next target.

SCOPE
  Two capacities, one mixture, one weight, single seed each, greedy decoding, one panel read as the
  first two content tokens. Exact for this panel; the conclusion is about binding at 19.9-33.8 M and
  does not claim a scaling law beyond that range.
