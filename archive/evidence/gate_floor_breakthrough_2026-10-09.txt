UOR-R4 — A serving-time gate floor at 0.5 recovers recall on every arm with ZERO losses; the identity arm becomes the best measured
Date: 2026-10-09. DeepSeek session. References #820. Follows gate_floor_run_card_2026-10-09.txt.

WHAT RAN
  The run card's experiment, exactly: an optional serving-time floor on the pointer's copy gate,
  gate' = max(gate, F), default 0 = bit-for-bit unchanged, applied at the two serving mixture sites only
  (greedy replies and the reported gate/mixture). NO training, no new parameters, no serialization
  change, no export change. Scored on existing checkpoints: arm-ptr (the best binding arm, 35/84),
  ptrident/arm-a-w05 (the identity arm whose gate collapsed to 0.1163, 10/84) and ptrident/arm-c-ctrl
  (the matched fine-tune, 24/84). 28 invented values, three conditions, 84 cells per arm per floor.

    arm-ptr        35/84 = 0.4167   49/84 = 0.5833    +14/84       p=1.22e-4   14 gained / 0 lost

THE MECHANISM IS CONFIRMED AND IT IS PURELY A MIXING WEIGHT
  Span mass and read mass are FLAT across F - bit-identical wherever the window is unchanged (arm-ptr
  0.8351 -> 0.8321; arm-a-w05 0.8399 -> 0.8392) - while the gate rises (0.5149 -> 0.6817; 0.1163 ->
  0.5089) and exact copies rise with it. On the 79 of 84 cells whose window is unchanged, the reported
  gate equals max(gate_F0, F) with ZERO mismatches and the generator, read and pointer masses are
  bit-identical. SO THE LEARNED ATTENTION ALREADY CONTAINED THE ANSWER AND ONLY ITS MIXING WEIGHT WAS
  WRONG. The identity term's damage was a serving-time miscalibration, not a loss of information.

THE SHAPE OF THE CURVE, AND A THIRD POSITION THE CARD DID NOT NAME
  The pre-registered monotonicity test FAILS: exact copies peak at F=0.5 and collapse afterwards
  (arm-ptr 35 -> 40 -> 49 -> 16 -> 6; arm-a-w05 10 -> 17 -> 59 -> 42 -> 26). As the card predicted,
  F=1.0 is far WORSE than F=0 on arm-ptr (-29/84, 1 gained / 30 lost, p=2.98e-8).
  WHY, MEASURED: at F=1.0 the gate is exactly 1.0000 and p_mixture of the value's first token is 0.8700,
  and the value is PRESENT in 79/84 replies (versus 55/84 at F=0) - yet exact copies are 6/84, because
  with the generator crowded out the replies degenerate: "Fennor.\n\nAss name is Fennor. Fennor.\n\nAss
  name is Fennor. Fennor.\n\nA"; "Candrake. name is Candrake. name is Candrake. ...". So ABOVE ~0.5 THE
  BINDING CONSTRAINT IS THE GENERATOR'S CONTRIBUTION TO THE REPLY'S SHAPE AND TERMINATION, not the
  value's identity. The card's dichotomy was incomplete and the third position is the one the data
  shows: the gate is the constraint only up to about half; beyond it the generator is needed for the
  reply to terminate and read as the value alone.

THE DEFAULT IS PROVABLY UNCHANGED

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE BINDING PROBLEM
  1. THE BEST MEASURED CONFIGURATION IS NOW: the identity-lineage arm with a serving-time gate floor of
     0.5, at 59/84 = 0.7024 exact recall of never-seen values, against 0.4167 for the previous best and
     0.0000 for the no-pointer control. That is a 69 % relative improvement over the previous best and
     it costs one constant, computed at inference, with no training.
  2. THE IDENTITY TERM SHOULD NOT BE WRITTEN OFF. Its loss was entirely a gate calibration problem: the
     same checkpoint goes from 0.1190 to 0.7024 by flooring the gate. The two negatives it produced
     (identity_score_negative, identity_term_control_refined) remain correct AS MEASURED - the term
     degrades an unfloored read-out - and are superseded in practice by this result.
  3. THE NEXT TARGET IS THE READ-OUT'S TERMINATION, not the gate and not the read: a conditional or
     decaying floor schedule, or an explicit stop rule on the pointer branch, is what the F=1.0
     degeneration points at. The gate curve says the useful band is around 0.5, and a flat floor is
     evidently the crudest member of that family.
