UOR-R4 — Correction and confirmation: the "Ana." panel item was a turn-1 echo; the pointer's copying IS value-driven, and a weak gate is what loses the answer
Date: 2026-10-09 (overnight rounds 171-173). DeepSeek session. References #820.

PART 1 - THE CORRECTION (my own claim, retracted in part)
  Round 171 recorded that the pointer arm's retention turn returned exactly "Ana.". THAT IS WRONG. The
  panel item I read is the ASSISTANT'S TURN-1 REPLY, and the document decodes as:

    <|bos|>User: My name is Ana.
           Assistant: Ana.<|eos|>          <- the "Ana." I reported; it ECHOES the user's own last word
           User: What is my name?
           Assistant: Ag.<|eos|>           <- THE RETENTION ANSWER, and it is NOT the value

  The panel re-run is bit-identical to the original on 14 of 14 rows at the same artifact sha256, so this
  is a reading error on my part, not a scoring discrepancy. "Ana." under a retention question appears
  ONLY under the pinned condition (turn 1 forced to " Sure."+EOS); in the natural condition and on the
  panel the retention turn is " Ag.".

PART 2 - WHAT THE VERIFICATION CONFIRMS ANYWAY: THE COPYING IS VALUE-DRIVEN
  Across seven rows in three conditions, on rows that differ ONLY in the value, the arm emits DIFFERENT
  replies that MATCH their own values:
    ben    -> " Ben."        zorba  -> " Zorba."       quillon -> " Quillon."      anvil -> " Anvil."
  Two of those values, ZORBA and QUILLON, appear in NO store family and NO plausible chat prior - the
  earlier arms produced "Zn."/"Zinc."/"Anany."/"Ane." in their place. And ana vs anvil, which share an
  onset, produce DIFFERENT strings. Measured magnitudes: pointer mass on the value's span 0.90-1.00 in 9
  of 21 cells; read-plus-pointer mass at or above TAU in 14 of 21; value-paired DELTA +3.85 to +10.39
  nats - against 1 of 7 argmax hits and 0 of 63 value-containing replies for the three commitment-weight
  arms. The failure class is c_readout in 14 of 21 cells. This is NOT the prior-driven class that the
  earlier "Anany."/"Ane." reading belonged to.

PART 3 - WHY THE ana ROW STILL LOSES, MEASURED
  On the ana rows the copy head sits at approximately 0.99 on the value's span - the read succeeds - while
  the LEARNED GATE is small, 0.07 to 0.11, so the generator outvotes the copy at the second token and the
  reply becomes " Ag." (shared onset id 439 plus the generator's "g"). On zorba, quillon and anvil the
  gates are 0.60 to 0.99 and the WHOLE SPAN is copied. The binding failure in this arm is therefore not
  retrieval, not the objective's weighting, and not the copy head: it is the GATE that decides whether
  the copy reaches the output, and it is weak exactly where the answer matters.

WHAT FOLLOWS, NAMED AND CHEAP
  1. SUPERVISE THE GATE. The review already identified the control: the project has pointer gate
     supervision (pointer_gate_supervision=) which was NOT used in this arm. With gates of 0.60-0.99 the
     span is copied whole (three rows demonstrate it); raising the gate on the answer positions is the
     direct intervention the measurement calls for.
  2. The count pair still fails everywhere (read mass below TAU on cats2/cats5 in all three conditions),
     so numeric recall is a separate gap from identity recall and should not be folded into the same claim.
  3. Any further claim must be read on the panel AND the probe, with the emitted-value test on rows whose
     values exist in no store family - which is how this correction was caught.

HONEST POSITION
  My round-171 claim was an over-read of a panel echo and is retracted here; the underlying mechanism it
  pointed at is nonetheless confirmed by this verification: a pointer on this mixture copies values it
  has never seen, value-conditionally, with the gate as the binding bottleneck. The night's solved result
  is unaffected (ten of ten form compliance on the demand-store configurations; served 0.93277
  bits/byte).
