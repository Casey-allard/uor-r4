## The change

**Scope.** `relation::read_features_window` (now a thin wrapper over the model-free `read_features_scan`) takes two distinct bounds: the committed-fact `cutoff`, a byte coordinate compared with each word's `byte_end`, and the current turn's input `boundary`, a token coordinate compared with each word's `end`, the same coordinate `historical_read::query_view` and the current-query handoff's `scoped_words` already use. Words are scanned most recent first and the first word outside the scope ends the scan. A matching owner's older neighbour that lies outside the scope contributes no lexical context (its address is masked to zero), so the before/after feature pair is drawn from the current request only. Stored records are untouched: the scope restricts which words may name the requested owner, not which records a legitimate current-owner lookup may read, and cross-turn memory works exactly as before (`Where is polru?` after a turn about `nemvi` reads `polru`'s record).

**Contract.** The changed semantics are a new versioned flag on the historical-version witness, `reader_turn_window`, which requires the earlier `reader_request_window` flag: legacy artifacts scan eight words with no bounds; `f3620cb7` keeps its measured post-fact-only scan; the new artifact adds the turn boundary. `Control::HistoricalVersionIntentReaderTurnScopeDisabled` withholds only the turn boundary (and reproduces `f3620cb7`'s leak, asserted in the artifact test), `HistoricalVersionIntentReaderWindowDisabled` returns to the eight-word scan, and removing the witness reproduces the frozen parent. `historical_version_intent::reader_scope` returns the three-part scope (window, post-fact cutoff, turn boundary) under every control, pinned by a unit test.

**Capacity.** `RELATION_FEATURES` is derived from the true maximum, `2 + 4 × WORD_QUERY` = 66, instead of the rounded 64; no match is dropped and the write stays unchecked-index-free by construction. Unit tests scan 0, 1, 8, 15 and 16 matching owner words in the full view and under the legacy eight-word base, a mixed flood, and a record whose owner never matches; the artifact test generates the review's sixteen-`selvi` flood under four controls and calls both public diagnostics on it. Buffers that receive the eight-word base elsewhere (96-slot arrays in dependent, handoff and historical readers) were audited: they still receive at most 34 base features.

**Retained artifact.** `15baec48` is `promote-turn-window` applied to `f3620cb7`: the learned witness, dictionary, codes and receipts of `4568679d` are unchanged through both promotions; no refit. No parser, owner/value allowlist, response template, evaluation-ID lookup, expected-answer fallback or host-side correction enters serving.

## Typed two-turn scope targets

Construction-4 adds a **`turn-scope` family of 1,080 typed two-turn targets** and 120 matched single-turn controls to construction-3. The first turn commits two owners' facts (forward or reverse layout, either order) and asks about one of them in three styles; the evaluated model generates that reply itself. The second turn asks about an **absent** owner (a deterministic fresh spelling), the **same** owner or the **competing** present owner, in five request forms, with no new fact, a third owner's new fact, or a repeated fact in the second turn (which moves that owner's live head to record 3). Absent-owner rows are a new typed target kind (`absent`): the exact answer is the parent's no-read abstention with no sourced answer; they are never labeled as learned abstentions for the witness, whose training documents keep `inherit` for them. Same and competing rows are typed current targets with the live head, complete value, present tense, declared form and frozen accepted lists (`answer_oracle::accepted` and `accepted_explanatory`), selected only at that record's own value endpoint. The matched controls carry the same facts and request in one prompt. The typed partition, API case export (absent rows included), disjoint counts and fresh draw all include these rows; failing rows stay in the denominator.
